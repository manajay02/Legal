Dhammika Ganepola, J.
When this matter was taken up for support on 07 October 2024, the
learned Counsel for the Petitioners made submissions in support of the
application. However, the learned Counsel for the 13th Respondent took
up several preliminary objections in relation to maintainability of this
application. The Petitioners in this application are inter alia seeking the
following reliefs:
 Writs of Certiorari quashing the quit notice [P19] issued by the
3rd Respondent;
 the 1st Respondent decision dated 18th March 2024[P11(ii)]
granting a permit to the 9th Respondent to cultivate the land
depicted as CC and A in the Sub Division Plan No.
Ka/BLT/14/521 dated 6th September 2016 and Sub Division Plan
No. KA/DSO/2020/502, and
 Writs of Mandamus directing the 1st Respondent to grant the
possession to the Petitioners of the land depicted in Lot 238 of
Village Plan No.140[P1] and also depicted as CC and A in the
Sub Division Plan No. Ka/BLT/14/521 dated 6th September
2016[P2] and Sub Division Plan No. KA/DSO/2020/502 [P3] and
 grant a long-term lease to the Petitioners in respect of the
property in subject.
The Petitioners state that they have been cultivating rubber and other
crops in Lot No.328 of P1 and Lot CC of P2 for more than 40 years. The
predecessor of the Petitioners, one Ranpati Pathirage Rajapaksha had
come into know that the said property is a State land and had taken steps
to legalize his possession in around 1990 making multiple requests to
various authorities. However, no inquiry has been held in respect of such
requests. The Petitioners state that the 1st Petitioner and the 2nd and 3rd
Petitioners [wife and the children of deceased] inherited the property in
subject after the demise of the said predecessor in 2020.
In support of the position that the Petitioners are in possession of the
subject property, the Petitioners have submitted several receipts
pertaining to payment of acres tax, receipts on payment of the rubber

--- Page 5 ---
replanting project, rubber replanting permits and rubber replanting
payment receipts in respect of the impugned property. Apart from
tendering such documents, the Petitioners also rely upon the permit
P11(ii) issued by 9th Respondent in invoking writ jurisdiction of this Court.
It appears that said permit P11(ii) has been issued by the 9th Respondent
to cultivate the land without conducting a formal inquiry into the requests
made by the Petitioners to obtain leasehold rights to the property.
The Petitioners assert that under the above circumstances, the materials
and evidence to form an opinions formed by the competent authority to
evict the Petitioners from the impugned property are wrongful and that
the decision to evict the Petitioners is unreasonable and irrational.
In the case of Udagedara Waththe Anusha KumarinNikaathagoda vs.
Jayasinghe Mudiyanselage Chamila Indika Jayasinghe, Divisional Secretary
and Others CA/WRIT/293/2017 decided on 18.11.2019 His Lordship Justice
Arjuna Obeyesekere has held;
This Court wishes to emphasize, for the avoidance of any doubt that
the Competent Authority is not required in terms of the Act to carry
out an inquiry of the title of the person who is in unauthorized
possession of such land.
The principle then is that while no inquiry is needed to form an
opinion, there should be a rational basis to form the opinion that the
State is lawfully entitled to the land. The rational basis should satisfy
the Wednesbury test of reasonableness. Thus, a Competent
Authority would be acting reasonably if he were acting on the basis
of a Surveyor Generals plan, even if the occupant is claiming
prescription. The Competent Authority is not expected to, and
indeed is precluded from, carrying out an inquiry
Accordingly, a requirement exists for the Court to consider whether there
was a rational basis for the competent authority to form an opinion that
the State is lawfully entitled to the land. In light of the above, the Court is
of the view that this Court should consider whether the competent
authority had sufficient material to arrive at such opinion and as to
whether the competent authority has taken into consideration relevant
matters when forming such opinions.
The Honorable Attorney General has taken up the stance that the instant
application should not be entertained as the material facts of the case are

in dispute. The Petitioners state that the 6th Respondent who drew up the
Subdivision Plan No. Ka/BLT/14/521 dated 6th September 2016 [P2] had
demarcated the property as inclusive of private property of the Petitioners.
It is stated that there is an ongoing dispute in respect of the property
between the Petitioners and the 9th Respondent before the District Court
of Matugama bearing No. L/5175/2024. However, it is important to
observe that the Petitioners in their written submissions have stated that
they do not wish to challenge the legality of the said plans in the instant
application and that no such relief has been sought in respect of the
legality of said plans.
Additionally, on behalf of the 13th Respondents, several preliminary
objections such as delay, futility and misrepresentation of material facts
have been taken up. However, the considering the circumstances and the
facts involved in the application, this Court is of the view that it is
appropriate to consider such objections at the stage of hearing.
Based on the above and the circumstances of the application I am inclined
to issue formal notices on the Respondents.
It is observed that unless this Court stay the operation of the quit notice
marked P19 until the final determination of this application, the
application will be rendered nugatory. Accordingly, I issue the stay order
suspending the operation of the quit notice P19 until the final
determination of this application.
