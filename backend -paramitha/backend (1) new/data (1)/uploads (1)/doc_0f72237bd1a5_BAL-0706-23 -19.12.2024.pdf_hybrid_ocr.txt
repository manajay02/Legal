K.M.G.H KULATUNGA, J.
1. This application for bail is preferred by the Petitioner on behalf of the
Accused Respondent under the provisions of Section 83(2) of the
Poisons, Opium, and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance. According to the
Petitioner, the suspect was arrested at her house on 07.10.2023. When
the Police arrived, another person (male) had escaped from the house.
It is common ground that the person who so left the house is Loku
Pathiranage Lahiru Rukshan the husband of the suspect. (vide-the B
report marked X-5) When he so retreated he has left behind his mobile
phone a jeep was also found in the compound of the house which
belongs to him. Therefore, it appears that the suspects husband had
in fact taken to his heels and escaped when the Police team approached
the house.
2. The suspect denies being in possession of any narcotics. However,
according to the Respondents, as depicted in the B Reports, the suspect
was having a parcel of heroin in a plastic bag in her hand when the
raiding team approached the house. It is also common ground that the
husband who escaped had been apprehended several days after whilst
being in possession of heroin. The portion marked X-10 of the B Report
X-5 also states that the said Loku Pathiranage Lahiru Rukshan has
previously been apprehended by several police stations in that area for
similar offences.
3. It was further submitted that the suspect has a child of three years,
who is said to be now cared for and looked after by her aging parents.
4. The learned State Counsel vehemently objected to this application, but
conceded that the indictment is yet to be forwarded as the Attorney
General is awaiting the IB Extracts.
5. The crux of the Petitioners submission is that it is improbable to expect
any ordinary person to hold a parcel containing 310 grammes of heroin
in her hand when a Police team is seen approaching the house. Her

--- Page 4 ---
husband who was there, appears to have taken to his heels just as the
raiding party was approached. In these circumstances, if the suspect
was conscious and knew that the parcel contained narcotics, it is highly
unlikely and improbable for her to have held on to it and keep it in her
hand. Therefore, the suspect was either unaware and ignorant as to the
contents of the bag or the B Reports do not narrate correctly the manner
of arrest and how she possessed the substance.
6. Secondly, the petitioner relies on the delay without instituting criminal
proceedings which is submitted to be excessive and oppressive. The
indictment or charges are yet to be preferred.
7. The arrest has taken place in October 2023. A period of one year and
two months has lapsed as at the date of the inquiry. The State Counsel
concedes the indictment is yet to be considered as the Attorney General
is yet to receive the IB extracts. The learned State Counsel is unable to
inform this Court as the present status of the IB extracts or of any
reason for the delay. This application for bail had been filed on
13.12.2023. The state had appeared on notice on 22.10.2024 on which
day this was fixed for the objections on 04.03.2024. However, in the
interim this was fixed for inquiry on 03.12.2024 and by letter dated
06.11.2024 the Hon. Attorney General was informed by the Registrar of
this Court that this will be taken up for inquiry on 03.12.2024 and has
also informed that objections if any be filed on or before 25.11.2024.
Accordingly, the matter was taken up for inquiry on 03.12.2024 on
which day, the State Counsel was heard. However, there was no
statement of objection filed of record. The State Counsel is unable to
inform as to a date or at least a probable time frame as to when charges
may be filed.
8. As such it is the opinion of this Court that the delay of one year and
two months considered in conjunctions with the said failure to explain
may be considered has been exceptional. It is well to remember that

--- Page 5 ---
when a person is denied of his liberty and held in detention those who
are responsible for the said deprivation should consciously and
diligently take necessary steps to diligently pursue with, finalize and
institute legal action. If a reasonable explanation for any delay is not
forthcoming such delay will be considered as being excessive or
oppressive. As held in Attorney General v. Ediriweera (S.C. Appeal
No. 100/2005), Delay is always a relative term and the question to be
considered is not whether there was mere explicable delay, as when
there is a backlog of cases, but whether there has been excessive or
oppressive delay and this always depends on the facts and
circumstances of the case.
9. Under section 83(2) of the Poisons Opium and Dangerous Drugs
Ordinance bail may be considered only on exceptional circumstances.
However, to my mind this extremely stringent provision of section 83
on granting of bail certainly cannot become or be allowed to be used as
a means to incarcerate an accused person without instituting action or
concluding the trial for an indefinite and unreasonably long time
without a just cause. Thus, time in remand when considered in
conjunction with the failure to take meaningful steps to institute action
and diligently prosecute to a finality in context may create the
circumstances that can be considered exceptional. The time spent in
remand cannot be considered in the abstract to determine if its
exceptional. Similarly, the normal delay which occurs between the
arrest and trial, cannot by itself amount to an exceptional
circumstance. I do not say that delay per se may not amount to an
exceptional circumstance. If there is inordinate and unexplained delay,
it may very well be.
10. It is common ground that the suspects estranged husband was present
and did escape and retreat when the police party was approaching. He
said to be a person with a previous history of drug related offences.
Then, on the other hand, there is an apparent improbability of the

--- Page 6 ---
suspect keeping in her hand a parcel with heroine when the police are
seen approaching and her husband retreats. As stated above, either she
may have been unaware as to the content or the version of the police
may be incorrect. Whatever be the reason this circumstance considered
with the unexplained delay in our view amounts to a circumstance that
can be considered exceptional. Accordingly, we are inclined to grant bail
to the suspect on the following conditions.
1. cash bail in a sum of Rs. 100,000.00;
2. to provide two sureties who should execute bonds to the value of
Rs. 500,000.00 each;
3. the sureties should provide certificates from the Grama Sevaka
and police reports from respective residential areas of such
sureties;
4. to surrender the passport or any travel document of the suspect,
if she has any, to the Registrar of the Magistrates Court of
Kuliyapitiya; and
5. report to the Officer-in-Charge of the Pannala Police Station on
every last Sunday of the month before 12 noon.
This Application is accordingly allowed and the Registrar of this
Court is directed to transmit a copy of this order to the Registrar
of the Magistrates Court of Kuliyapitiya.
