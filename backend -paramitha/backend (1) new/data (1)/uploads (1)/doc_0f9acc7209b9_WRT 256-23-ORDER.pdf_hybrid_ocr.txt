The facts of this case are briefly as follows:
According to the petition, the Petitioner is a company duly incorporated under the
Companies Act No. . The Petitioner states that, on or about 31.07.2020, the 4th
Respondent who was a former employee of the Petitioner had made a complaint to the
Department of Labour alleging that a sum of Rs. 34,080,000/- was not paid by the
Petitioner to the 4th Respondent as gratuity. The Petitioner was notified by letter dated

--- Page 5 ---
03.09.2020 of an inquiry to be held by the 3rd Respondent in respect of the said complaint.
In the said inquiry, parties were directed to file their written submissions.
The Petitioner states that the 4th Respondent filing an undated written submission put
forward the position that the 4th Respondent having worked for the Petitioner for 16 years
was drawing a final salary of Rs. 2,130,000/-. Therefore, he is entitled for a sum of Rs.
34,080,000/- with a surcharge of Rs. 10,224,000/- which in total amounts to Rs.
44,304,000/- out of which the 4th Respondent has obtained a loan for a sum of Rs.
31,235,000/- and accordingly a sum of Rs. 13,069,000/- is payable as gratuity to the 4th
Respondent from the Petitioner. The Petitioner states that by the written submission
dated 10.11.2020, he informed that, since the 4th Respondent had a break of service, the
4th Respondent is only entitled to a sum of Rs. 5,262,000/-. The Petitioner further states
that, however, as the 4th Respondent had obtained a loan amounting to a sum of Rs.
31,235,000/- from the Petitioner against the gratuity payable to the 4th Respondent to be
settled, there was no gratuity payable or due to the 4th Respondent by the Petitioner.
The Petitioner avers that, by a letter dated 06.09.2022, the 2nd Respondent informed the
Petitioner that a sum of Rs. 6,847,750/- is payable to the 4th Respondent as unpaid gratuity
and surcharge and further that if the Petitioner has already paid the said sum to the 4th
Respondent, the Petitioner should inform the same with proof thereof to the 2nd
Respondent. In response, the Petitioner by letter 14.09.2022 informed the 2nd Respondent
that, on the request of the 4th Respondent, the Petitioner has already paid the gratuity
and therefore, no surcharge or outstanding amount is payable to the 4th Respondent by
the Petitioner. Further, through this letter, the Petitioner sought a written directive from
the 2nd Respondent that the Petitioner has already paid the gratuity to the 4th Respondent.

--- Page 6 ---
The 2nd Respondent by letter dated 04.10.2022 refused the Petitioners application on the
basis that the gratuity cannot be deducted from a loan as there is no provision for such
effect provided by law.
The main contention of the Petitioner is that the determination of the 2nd Respondent
directing the Petitioner to make the payment of gratuity to the 4th Respondent together
with the surcharge is unlawful and unreasonable.
In this context, the Petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking inter
alia writs of Certiorari quashing the determination of the 2nd Respondent marked P6.
On the other hand, the 1st to 3rd Respondents state in their written submission dated
12.11.2024 that according to Section 5 (1) of the Payment of Gratuity Act No.
as amended (hereinafter referred to as the Act), an employee is entitled to gratuity in the
instances of termination, retirement, death or operation of law or otherwise. It is their
submission, therefore, an employee who is in service is unable to obtain a loan against his
gratuity as such employee is entitled to such gratuity only upon ceasing to be an employee.
In this context, this Court focuses on Section 5 (1) of the Act which reads thus;
Every employer who employs or has employed fifteen or more workmen on any
day during the period of twelve months immediately preceding the termination of
the services of a workman in any industry shall, on termination (whether by the
employer or workman, or on retirement or by the death of the workman, or by
operation of law, or otherwise) of the services at any time after the coming into
operation of this Act, of a workman who has a period of service of not less than five
completed years under that employer, pay to that workman in respect of such
services, and where the termination is by the death of that workman, to his heirs,
a gratuity computed in accordance with the provisions of this Part within a period
of thirty days of such termination.

--- Page 7 ---
The above Section clearly depicts that gratuity is to be paid to the workman by the
employer immediately preceding the termination of the services of a workman.
Accordingly, in the instant application, the Petitioners contention that the 4th Respondent
has obtained a loan against his gratuity prior to his termination does not stand.
The question before this Court is how gratuity should be calculated. According to Section
6(2)(b) of the Act, when computing the gratuity, the last drawn salary is the basis on
which such gratuity is calculated.
A workman referred to in subsection. (1) of section 5 shall be entitled to receive
as gratuity, a sum equivalent to-
(a) half a month's, wage or salary for each year of completed service
computed at the rate of wage or salary last drawn by the workman, in the
case of a monthly rated workman; and
In the instant case, the 4th Respondent retired from his work on 30.11.2016. The
contention of the Petitioner is that the Petitioner has paid the gratuity by way of a loan
while the 4th Respondent was in service.
It should be noted that there is no provision in the Act that a loan can be granted against
the gratuity due to him. In fact, gratuity is available on retirement based on his last drawn
salary.
The only provision available in the said Act that estop paying such gratuity is under
Section 13 of the Act which reads thus:
Any workman, to whom a gratuity is payable under Part II of this Act and, whose
services have been terminated for reasons of fraud, misappropriation of funds of
the employer, willful damage to property of the employer, or causing the loss of

--- Page 8 ---
goods, articles or property of the employer, shall forfeit such gratuity to the extent
of the damage or loss caused by him.
In the instant application, the Petitioner has disclosed that an action was filed in the
Magistrate Court of Colombo Case No. 85512/2/22 under Section 136 (1) (a) of the
Criminal Procedure Code against the 4th Respondent for the abetment of offences under
Section 391 (Criminal Breach of Trust) and Section 467 (Falsification of Accounts) of the
Penal Code on the basis of the actions of the 4th Respondent in obtaining the advance
payment of gratuity in a sum that is excessively over and above the sum actually payable
as gratuity to the 4th Respondent.
Nevertheless, it should be noted that the 4th Respondent was not terminated from his
service on this ground. In the instant case, thus, the Petitioner has not indicated that
there is any forfeiture of gratuity as such which comes within the scope of Section 13 of
the Act.
Furthermore, this Court notes that the 4th Respondent has indicated to the Court that the
Petitioner has failed to disclose that the Petitioners Sister Company, Micro Cars (Pvt)
Ltd has instituted an action in the District Court of Mahara case bearing No.
165/2023/DMR against the 4th Respondent to recover the loan granted to the 4th
Respondent.
The 1st and 2nd Respondents have taken steps under Section 8 (1) of the Act to recover the
sum due as gratuity from the Petitioner by filing a certificate after the inquiry.
The main ground urged by the Petitioner is that the determination of the 2nd Respondent
in the document marked P6, directing for the petitioner to make a payment of Rs.
6,847,750/- as gratuity and surcharge to the 4th Respondent and by filing the certificate
instituting action in the Magistrates Court are illegal and ultra vires.

--- Page 9 ---
I hold that by virtue of Sections 5 and 8 of the Gratuity Act, the Commissioner of Labour
is vested with the jurisdiction to recover the gratuity payment.
We further hold that gratuity will be applicable only after the termination of service of a
workman or death of a workman.
The Petitioner has failed to establish a prima facie case to issue writs of Certiorari in
terms of the Atkinian formula.
For the above-mentioned reasons, we dismiss the action with costs of Rs. 50,000/-.
