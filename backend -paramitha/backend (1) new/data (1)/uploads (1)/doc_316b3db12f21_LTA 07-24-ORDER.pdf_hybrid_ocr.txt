B. Sasi Mahendran, J.
This leave to appeal application emanates from the Order of the Wakf Tribunal dated
06.04.2024 where the Tribunal appointed a receiver, removing the Trustees from the
Management and required the Petitioners who are the Trustees to hand over the possession
of the property at Maharagama to the said receiver.
This application deals with the N.D.H. Abdul Ghaffoor Trust established by the Deed of Trust
No. 2125 dated 21.11.1935 attested by John Wilson, Notary Public of Colombo. This Deed of
Trust involves two properties namely a plantation land (comprising of 3 buildings on one end
thereof and a plantation on the remainder) of a total of 17 Acres in extent, situated at
Maharagama and more fully described in the First Scheule of the said Trust Deed and a
prime commercial land of approximately 386 perches (2  acres) situated in Grandpass,
Colombo 14 and more fully described in the Second Scheule of the said Trust Deed.
The Petitioners state that several old boys of the Gaffooriya Arabic College which is a
property described in the First Scheule of the said Trust Deed have made several applications
against the trustees of the said Trust in the Wakf Tribunal.
The Petitioners state that the Plaintiff- Respondents who are claiming to have interest in the
said Trust, filed a plaint in the Wakf Tribunal in case bearing No. WT/297/2023 inter alia
seeking as one of the prayers to appoint a receiver for the better custody and management of

--- Page 9 ---
the properties described in the Trust. The Tribunal delivered its order dated 06.04.2024
marked as P23 inter alia appointing a receiver by removing the Trustees from the
Management and requiring the Petitioners to hand over the possession of the property at
Maharagama to the said receiver.
The Petitioners have preferred the present leave to appeal application against the said order
of the Wakf Tribunal dated 06.04.2024 marked as P23 to set aside the same and seek an
interim order staying the operations of the said order and the proceedings of the Wakf
Tribunal Case bearing No. WT/297/2023.
We are mindful that a number of cases have been filed against the trustees in the Wakf
Tribunal. Against the orders of the said Wakf Tribunal, several cases have been filed in the
Court of Appeal to quash the said orders.
In LTA/06/2021 connected with the Wakf Tribunal Case No. WT/273/2020 which deals with
the same Trust Deed where the Petitioners in this Case have challenged the order of the
Wakf Tribunal dated 04.02.2021 and sought an interim order on the basis that the said Deed
of Trust bearing No. 2125 is not a Muslim Charitable Trust. For convenience, the said order
of the Court of Appeal is reproduced below.
This application is filed by 1 to 11" Respondent Petitioners among several other
reliefs seeking to impugn the order of the Wakfs Tribunal dated 40 . February 2021
('P9*). The Respondent-Petitioners also sort the interim - and/or stay orders prayed
for in prayer (f), (g), (i), (), (k) and (1) of the Petition. This Court has already issued
the interim order prayed for in prayer (g) of the Petition, on 10" February 2022 and
has been extended from time to time.

--- Page 10 ---
Having carefully considered the submissions made by the learned Counsel for the
Appellant, learned State Counsel for the 1st Respondent and the learned Counsel for
the 3rd to 7th Respondents, we are of the view that, prima facie there is a serious
question of law need to be, gone into by this Court as to whether the Deed of Trust
bearing No. 2125 dated 21st November 1935, attested by John Wilson, Notary Public
(PI') is or not a Muslim Charitable Trust or Wakf.
Consequently, the issue arises as to whether the appointment of the new Management
Committee of Ghaffooriya Arabic College is lawful, and also whether the Wakf
Tribunal has jurisdiction to accept, hear and determine the application made by the
applicant respondent and to make the impugned order marked P9
Accordingly, we formally issue notice on the respondent's name in the petition of this
court.
Further, in order to maintain the status quo, we issue orders prayed for in paragraphs
(f), (g),( i),( j), (k), and (l) until the final determination of this application.
According to the above-said order, the Court of Appeal has decided that there is a serious
question to be looked into whether the particular trust is or is not a Muslim Charitable Trust
and issued a stay order staying the further proceedings of the Tribunal.
We are mindful that the Wakf Tribunal has jurisdiction only if the particular trust comes
under the Muslim Charitable Trust according to Section 9J of the Muslim Mosques and
Charitable Trusts or Wakfs Act No. as amended.
Despite this order, the Tribunal has affirmed that the Trust established by Deed of Trust No.
2125 is a Muslim Charitable Trust and proceeded to appoint a receiver.

--- Page 11 ---
When the matter was supported, the Counsel for the Petitioners brought to the notice of the
Court the following facts.
A) Knowing the fact that the cases are pending before the Court of Appeal regarding whether
the Trust involved is a Muslim Charitable Trust or not which is a jurisdictional issue, the
Tribunal has proceeded to act on the basis that it is a Muslim Charitable Trust. The
jurisdiction of the Tribunal is limited only to the Muslim Trusts. But the main objection
taken by the Petitioner is that the said Trust is a Private Trust, therefore, the Tribunal
has no jurisdiction to hear the matter.
B) According to Section 671 of the Civil Procedure Code, the appointment of the receiver
should be done by the Court and no one else. For the purpose of convenience, the said
Section 671 is reproduced below.
Whenever it appears to the court to be necessary for the restoration, preservation, or
better custody or management of any property, movable or immovable, the subject of
an action, or under sequestration, the court may on the application of any party who
shall establish a prima facie right to or interest in such property, by order
(a) appoint a receiver of such property, and, if need be,
(b) remove the person, in whose possession or custody the property may be,
from the possession or custody thereof;
(c) commit such property to the custody or management of such receiver; and
(d) grant to such receiver such fee or commission on the rents and profits of the
property by way of remuneration as the court thinks fit, and all such powers
as to bringing and defending actions and for the realization, management,
protection, preservation, and improvement of the property, the collection of the

--- Page 12 ---
rents and profits thereof, the application and disposal of such rents and profits,
and the execution of instruments in writing, as the owner himself has, or such
of those powers as the court thinks fit.
We are mindful that the Tribunal has directed the Respondents to recommend a name which
means that the Tribunal has acted in contravention of Section 671 of the Civil Procedure
Code which directs the Court to appoint the receiver of the Trust. An excerpt of the order of
the Tribunal dated 06.04.2024 is reproduced below for easy reference.
We therefore, allow this application for the appointment of a Receiver and make order
removing the trustees, the 1st to the 12th defendants, from the management and
custody of the Gaffooriya Arabic College and the property at Maharagama and appoint
one of the persons named by the plaintiffs as Receiver, in terms of section 671 of the
Civil Procedure Code to manage the said Arabic College, which is the subject matter
of this case.
The Tribunal has delegated its powers to Respondents to nominate a receiver. The power
conferred on the authority (the Tribunal) by statute must be lawfully exercised by the
authority itself. This principle is discussed in Wade and Forsyths Administrative Law,
Twelfth Edition at page 255 - 257;
Delegation
Inalienable Discretionary Power
An element which is essential to the lawful exercise of power is that it should be
exercised by the authority upon whom it is conferred, and by no one else. The principle
is strictly applied, even where it causes administrative inconvenience, except in cases
where it may reasonably be inferred that the power was intended to be delegable.

--- Page 13 ---
Normally the courts are rigorous in requiring the power to be exercised by the precise
person or body stated in the statute, and in condemning as ultra vires action taken by
agents, sub-committees or delegates, however expressly authorised by the authority
endowed with the power.
One aspect of this principle is the rule that the participation of non-members in the
deliberations or decisions of a collective body may invalidate its acts. The decision of a
disciplinary committee, for example, is likely to be invalid if any non-member of the
committee has taken part in its proceedings. It is not clear that the mere presence of a
non-member will be fatal, although in one case Lord Wright MR said:
It would be most improper on general principles of law that extraneous
persons, who may or may not have independent interests of their own, should
be present at the formulation of that judicial decision.
Therefore, the question is whether the Tribunal has the power to delegate.
C) When the receiver was appointed, there was no quorum as required by Section 9D (3) of
the Muslim Mosques and Charitable Trusts or Wakfs Act No. as amended
which reads as follows;
Two members of the Tribunal shall form a quorum. No matter shall be heard
by the Tribunal unless a quorum is present.
We hold that when the receiver was appointed by the Chairman of the Wakf Tribunal, the
quorum required by the statute was not met. Our Courts have considered that failure to meet
the quorum as required by the statute will go to the root of the matter.

--- Page 14 ---
In Anoma S. Polwatte v. Ms. L. Jayawickrama and Others SC WRIT Application No. 01/2011
decided on 27.07.2018, His Lordship Vijith K. Malalgoda PC, J held that;
When looking at the provisions of the above three provisions of the Act it is clear that
by the above provisions, a clear distinction had been made between the powers of the
Commission and functions of the Commission. As identified in section 3 referred to
above, when an offence is disclosed after an Investigation, Commission shall direct
the institution of proceedings and the said conduct of the Commission had been
identified within the Functions of the Commission. The powers of the Commission has
been identified under section 5 of the Act and under section 2 (8), such powers of the
Commission may exercise by its members either sitting together or separately.
Thus it is clear that the members of the Commission can exercise ancillary powers on
his own though the full complement of the Commission is not available at one given
time. But as for the exercise of functions such as the direction to be given to the
Director General, it is crystal clear that the Act has not provided for one member alone
to give such direction. However as already observed by me, R-1 refers to a directive
given by one member and in the said directive, it is not clear as to whether the reports
referred to, had been considered by the full commission before making such directive.
In the said circumstance, I have no hesitation to conclude that there is no valid
directive made under section 11 of the Act to institute criminal proceedings before the
Magistrates Court, with regard to the investigations carried out by the fraud
Investigation Unit of the Commission to Investigate Bribery and Corruption as
against the Petitioner to the present Application. I therefore conclude the directive
produced marked R-1 is patently illegal.

In Associated Motorways (Pvt) Ltd. v. Consumer Affairs Authority of Sri Lanka and Another
CA/WRIT/430/2015 decided on 16.11.2020, His Lordship Mahinda Samayawardhena, J. (as
he was then) held that;
Before I consider the Petitioners case on its merits, there is a preliminary issue to be
addressed, though technical in nature, which goes to the root of the matter. That is,
the validity of the impugned decision due to lack of quorum.
Item 8(2) of the Schedule to the Consumer Affairs Authority Act (read with section
3(4) of the Act) says The quorum for any meeting of the Authority shall be four
members. Learned Presidents Counsel for the Petitioner submits that when the
impugned decision A18B was purportedly made on 27.11.2014, there was no quorum
of the Authority in the eyes of the law to make such a decision.
Further held that;
For the aforesaid reasons I hold that the impugned decision A18B is a nullity, as it
has not been made by a properly constituted panel of inquiry as required by law. In
other words, the decision has no force or avail in law due to the lack of quorum.
Therefore, in this instant case, the question is when the Chairman appointed the receiver
whether there was the quorum required by the statute.
In addition, the allegation made by the Plaintiffs was of mismanagement of the Trust. We
are mindful that before appointing the receiver, the Tribunal should have asked the
Petitioners to submit the accounts relating to the Trust to satisfy themselves of the allegation
made against the Petitioners. Simply appointing a receiver on the allegation made by the
Plaintiffs is not expected from the Tribunal. Before the Tribunal appointed the person, they
should have asked for the proper accounts to be produced.

--- Page 16 ---
Therefore, the issue arises as to whether the appointment of the receiver is lawful.
Accordingly, we grant the leave to appeal against the order dated 06.04.2024 made by the
Wakf Tribunal and we issue notice on all the Respondents named in the Petition to this
Court.
Further, in order to maintain the status quo, we issue orders prayed for in paragraphs (e),
(f), (g) (h) and (i) until the final determination of this application.
