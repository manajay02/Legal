ORDER
P. Kumararatnam, J.
This is an application in Revision preferred by the 1st Accused-
Petitioner (Hereinafter referred to as the Petitioner) challenging the
conviction and the sentence imposed by the Learned High Court Judge
of Badulla on 04.01.2024 against him.

The Petitioner was indicted in the High Court of Badulla along with 2nd
Accused for criminal offences allegedly to have been committed on
10.08.2003. The charges in the indictment are re-produced bellow:
1. For committing robbery of a van bearing No. UP HF 4800 which
was worth about Rs.1,100,000/- and belonging to one Mahinda
Wijesinghe Gunawardena constitute an offence punishable under
Section 380 read with Section 32 of the Penal Code.
2. For committing the murder of Mahinda Wijesinghe Gunawardena
which constitute an offence punishable under Section 296 read
with Section 32 of the Penal Code.
The indictment was read over to the Petitioner and the 2nd Accused on
26.07.2010 and trial commenced subsequently. During the trial, the
prosecution had called 16 witnesses and marked productions P1 to
P18.
The learned High Court Judge had called for the defence having
satisfied that the prosecution had established a prima facie case
against the Petitioner and the 2nd Accused. The Petitioner and the 2nd
Accused made dock statements and closed their case.
After the submissions by the prosecution and the defence, the Learned
High Court Judge had convicted the Petitioner and the 2nd Accused for
the 1st Count and acquitted them from the 2nd Count. Thereafter, the
learned High Court Judge had sentenced them as follows:
Count 1
10 years rigorous imprisonment with fine of Rs. 15000/-. In default
12 months simple imprisonment.
Being aggrieved by the conviction and the sentence the Petitioner had
filed this revision application to this Court.

The Petitioner had urged following reasons for filing this application
before this Court.
A. The Learned Trial Judge has erred in law by failing to consider
that the Prosecution had not proved the ingredients of the
offence of robbery which is described in section 379 of the Penal
Code.
B. The Learned Trial Judge has erred in law by failing to consider
Section 114 (a) of the Evidence Ordinance in the correct
perspective that a man who is in possession of stolen goods soon
after the theft is either the thief or has received the goods
knowing them to be stolen unless he can account for his
possession.
C. The Learned Trial Judge has erred in law by arriving at the
conclusion that the accused committed of offence of robbery due
to the fact that the van was in the possession of the Accused.
D. In any event, the sentence imposed on the accused is excessive
in the light of the facts and the circumstances of the case.
The Petitioner was acquitted from the charge of murder. As there was
no explanation with regard to the possession of stolen property, the
Petitioner argue that the prosecution should have amended the
indictment and convicted the Petitioner for the offence of theft or a
lesser offence. Further, the Petitioner had no previous conviction or
pending cases when he was convicted for charge of robbery. Hence, the
Petitioner submits, due to this, a serious miscarriage of justice has
occasioned and seek this Courts intervention in the exercise of the
extraordinary jurisdiction by way of revision.

It is well settled law that the revision is a discretionary remedy that can
be granted only upon exceptional circumstances. The Petitioner
submits that items set out in paragraph 9 and 11 of the petition
constitute exceptional circumstances. The main complaint is that that
the Petitioner should have been convicted for a lesser offence.
In Wijesinghe v Tharmaratnam (Sri kantha Law Report Vol-IV 47), the
court held that:
Revision is a discretionary remedy and will not be available
unless the application discloses circumstances which shocks the
conscience of the Court.
In Hotel Galaxy (Pvt) Ltd. V Mercantile Hotels Management Ltd.
1987 1 SLR 5 the Court held that:
It is settled law that the exercise of the revisionary powers of the
appellate Court is confined to cases in which exceptional
circumstances exist warranting its intervention.
The reasons adduced for delay of 08 months to file this revision
application cannot be accepted by this Court. Further, the Petitioner
had not taken any endeavour to file an appeal against the conviction
and the sentence. Had he really wanted to lodge an appeal against
judgment of the learned High Court Judge of Badulla, he could have
done it through the prison.
Hence, considering the seriousness of the offence and the punishment
imposed on the Petitioner by the Learned High Court Judge, I consider
this is not an appropriate case to issue notice to the Respondent.

Considering all the factors mentioned above, I conclude this is not an
