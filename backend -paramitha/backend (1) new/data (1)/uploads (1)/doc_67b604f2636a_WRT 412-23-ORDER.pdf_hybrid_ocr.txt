ORDER
This order pertains to whether the notice and interim reliefs are to be issued.
The Petitioner instituted this action by way of a petition dated 06.07.2023 seeking the
following reliefs:
a. Issue Notice on the IS to 17th Respondents above named;
b. Issue a Mandate in the nature of a Writ of Certiorari, quashing the said notice or
certificate of the 1st and 2nd Respondents and issue certified copies of the inquiry
held before the 2nd Respondent;
c. Make a direction the 2nd and 3rd Respondents to issue certified copies of the all
documents contained in the inquiry file
d. Issue an Interim Order, staying the operation of the said notice or certificate and/or
restraining the 1st and 2nd Respondents from compelling the Petitioner to pay
default EPF payment or any part thereof, as per and/or in terms of the said notice
or certificate, subject to such terms and/or conditions and or limitations and/or
qualifications as to Your Lordships' Court may seem meet, pending the hearing
and final determination of the above styled Application;
e. Grant Costs;
f. Grant such other and further relief as to Your Lordships' Court may seem meet.
The facts of this case are briefly as follows:

--- Page 5 ---
According to the petition, the Petitioner is a duly incorporated company engaged in the
business of manufacturing and distribution of springs. The Petitioner states that the 4th
to 17th Respondents have made a complaint to the 2nd Respondent that they were deprived
of the Employee Provident Fund contribution (hereinafter referred to as EPF) for the
period commencing from January 2010 to April 2013 which was denied by the Petitioner
that the said Respondents were not employed by the Petitioner during the said period.
After an inquiry, the 2nd Respondent directed the Petitioner to pay a sum of Rs.
2,142,008.47/- as the defaulted EPF contribution. The 2nd Respondent has filed a
certificate dated 28.10.2014 before the Magistrates Court of Kandy to recover the same.
The Petitioner avers that the Petitioner requested certified copies of the inquiry led before
the 2nd Respondent by letter dated 12.01.2021 which was denied by the 2nd Respondent
informing to direct such request to Additional Commissioner General of Labour (Admin).
The Additional Commissioner thereafter informed that such information cannot be
provided directly but can only be accommodated on an order of the Magistrates Court of
Kandy.
Consequent to such denial, the Petitioner again requested the information to invoke the
jurisdiction of this Court by letter dated 13.02.2022 marked as P6 to which the 3rd
Respondent replied that since the Magistrates Court of Kandy has already delivered an
order on the said Certificate, the Petitioner has no legal entitlement to invoke the
jurisdiction of this Court, thus refusing such information. The Petitioner being aggrieved
by the said response of the 3rd Respondent appealed to the High Court of Kandy against
the said order of the Magistrate Court.
In this context, the Petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking inter
alia a writ of Certiorari to quash the said notice or certificate of the 1st and 2nd
Respondents.
It should be noted that this application was filed on 20.07.2023 after the order of the
Learned Magistrate was delivered against the Petitioner directing him to pay the due
sum. But, the Petitioner has failed to move this Court to quash the said decision. The only
ground urged by the Petitioner is to quash the certificate issued by the 1st and 2nd
Respondents and direct the 1st and 2nd to issue certified copies of the inquiry held.
Once the Commissioner of Labour files the certificate under Section 38 (2) of the
Employees Provident Fund Act No. as amended (hereinafter referred to as the
Act) to recover the EPF contribution that the employer has failed to pay to the Fund, then

--- Page 6 ---
the Magistrate has the power to recover the arrears of the said contribution. In the instant
case, before filing this action, the Learned Magistrate has issued summons to the
Petitioner asking him to show cause with regard to the recovery of the said sum. We are
mindful that the certificate issued to the Magistrate is the last resort. The Act provides
the employer the final opportunity to show cause before the magistrate that the sum is
not lawfully due. The Petitioner has failed to inform in the petition, that the Magistrate
has not given him an opportunity to show cause. I am of the view that the Petitioner has
suppressed material facts before this Court. Further, we note that the Petitioner has
failed to aver the details of the order made by the Learned Magistrate.
In the particular inquiry before the Learned Magistrate, he should have established that
the Petitioner is not a defaulter. By delivering the order under Section 38(2) of the said
Act, the particular sum shall be deemed to be a fine imposed as a sentence of the
Magistrate. In other words, the Court has declared the Petitioner to be a defaulter. The
said order is not before this Court and is not challenged in this application.
We are mindful that even if the main relief sought by the Petitioner which is to quash the
certificate made by the 1st and 2nd Respondents is to be granted, such would be futile as
the Learned Magistrate of Kandy has already decided on the said Certificate that the
Petitioner is a defaulter.
This Court has followed that writs which are a discretionary remedy will not be issued if
the Court is of the opinion granting such writs is futile.
In Sethu Ramasamy v. A.E.G. Moregoda, 63 NLR 115 at page 117, Gunasekara, J held
that;
 This view of the matter, however, cannot conclude the question whether the
application should be granted; for, as has been pointed out by Mr. Jayararatne, a
mandamus will not be granted when it appears that it would be futile in its result.
In M.M. Shamsudeen, v. The Minister of Defence and External Affairs, 63 NLR 430 at
page 432, L.B. De Silva, J held that;
 The issue of a writ of mandamus is within the discretion of Court and will not be
issued if it will be futile to do so. In this case I am satisfied that the petitioner has
failed to prove that his mother was a citizen of Ceylon by descent.

--- Page 7 ---
The above judgments were considered by His Lordship Sisira de Abrew, J in Selvamani
v. Dr. Kumaraveluppillai and Others, ( 2002) (1) SLR 99 at page 103 where it was held
that;
The petitioner has now vacated post. This is evident from letter dated 25.08.2004
(3R9A). The petitioner has been sent on vacation of post after the decision in P4A.
At the hearing of this application the learned Counsel for the petitioner admitted
that no application for writ of certiorari has been filed to quash the said order
whereby the petitioner was sent on vacation of post.
The petitioner by this application also moves for a writ of mandamus on the first
respondent directing that the petitioner be restored to his earlier position i. e. to
the post of Project Operator. Even if this application of the petitioner is granted,
he is not entitled to resume his earlier office in view of the order of vacation of post
(3R9A). Therefore, issuing a writ of mandamus in this case would be futile.
We hold that, therefore, quashing the certificate filed by the 1st and 2nd Respondents is
futile in view of the order of the learned Magistrate of Kandy.
Application dismissed with costs of Rs. 50,000/-
