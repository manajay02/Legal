MOHAMMED LAFFAR, J.
The Petitioner in this application has invoked the Writ jurisdiction of this Court
under Article 140 of the Constitution seeking several reliefs as prayed for in the
prayers to the Petition. After hearing the learned Counsel for the Petitioner and
the learned State Counsel for the Respondents, this Court on 192022 issued

--- Page 3 ---
notices on the Respondents only in reference to the paragraphs (d) and (e) ii of
the prayers to the Petition, which reads thus.
(d) Issue a mandate in the nature of a Writ of Prohibition preventing any further
steps or action in pursuance of the purported determination of the 1st Respondent
published by Notice dated 191980 marked P9, purporting to assume
possession of the Petitioners lands forming the subject matter of this application,
containing an extent of 27.60 perches duly conveyed by Deed of Transfer No.
795 marked P2 (a), as more fully described in the schedule hereto and depicted
on plan No. 7657 marked P2 (b) as lots 1A, 1B and 1C, situated at No. 35/10,
1st Lane, Quarry Road, Mirihana.
(e) ii. Compelling the 1st Respondent to act under S 39A of the Land Acquisition
Act and divest the Petitioners property forming the subject matter of this
application, containing an extent of 27.60 perches duly conveyed by Deed of
Transfer No. 795 marked P2 (a), as more fully described in the schedule hereto
and depicted on plan No. 7657 marked P2 (b) as lots 1A, 1B and 1C, situated at
No. 35/10, 1st Lane, Quarry Road, Mirihana.
In terms of the Petition, the subject land is depicted as lots 1A, 1B and 1C in
Plan No. 7657 dated 192009 made by Gamini Dodanwela, Licensed
Surveyor marked as P2b. The 6th Respondent, the purported owner of the
aforesaid land in dispute had transferred the same to the Petitioner by deed
bearing No. 795 dated 252020 attested by T.S. Amarasiri, Notary Public
marked as P2a. The Petitioner asserts that his land along with a larger land was
acquired by the state for the purpose of constructing a new Parliament
Complex. The said Parliament Complex has been successfully constructed and
it does not include the said land purchased by the Petitioner. In these
circumstances, the Respondents identified the land in dispute for the purpose
of constructing a Bird Park. As such, it is contended that the subject land that
has been acquired for the public purpose of constructing a new Parliament
Complex cannot be utilized for some other purpose. In this scenario, the
Petitioner seeks the aforesaid reliefs from this Court.
It is evident that the Notice under section 38A of the Land Acquisition Act in
respect of the land in dispute was published in the Gazette on 19-051980 (P9).
All 23 lots depicted in the Preliminary Plan No. 5529 dated 141981 (P11)
have been acquired. Notice under section 7 of the said Act has been published
in the Gazette on 231982 (P10). It is borne out from the documents marked
as R1 and R2 that the compensation for claimants had been paid in 1987. The
3rd Respondent, Divisional Secretary, Maharagama in her affidavit dated 04
2022 affirmed that compensation had been paid in full in respect of the land

--- Page 4 ---
acquired. Thereafter, on 281987, under section 44 (1) of the said Act, the
subject land had been vested with the Urban Development Authority (R3). In
these respects, it appears to this Court that the Petitioner is challenging the
validity of the acquisition that was concluded 23 years ago from the date of the
institution of this application.
Thus, it is an undisputed fact that there is a long delay on the part of the
Petitioner in invoking the Writ jurisdiction of this Court. In this regard, I refer
to the observation made by the Supreme Court in the case of D.D. Kaluarachchi
Vs. Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (SC. Appeal No. 43/2013, SC Minute dated
192019). This is the case where five years after their retirement, four former
employees of the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation filed an Application for Writs
before the Court of Appeal and moved Court for Writs of Certiorari and
Mandamus in respect of certain salary arrears. The Court of Appeal issued a
Writ of Mandamus directing the Ceylon Petroleum Corporation to pay the
Petitioners certain salary arrears. Murdu Fernando, J. agreeing with Sisira de
Abrew and Vijith Malalgoda JJ., observed that;
I am inclined to accept the contention of the Appellants that the Court of
Appeal should have dismissed this Application in-limine on the ground of laches
which was a threshold issue. The Court of Appeal did not consider the grounds
of laches, which was raised as a preliminary objection. I observe this omission as
a grave error in the Court of Appeal Judgment..
In Bisomenike Vs. C. R. de Alwis (1982-1SLR-368), Sharvananda, J., (as he
then was) observed that,
"a Writ of Certiorari is issued at the discretion of the Court. It cannot be held to
be a Writ of right or one issued as a matter of course. The exercise of this
discretion by Court is governed by certain well-accepted principles. The Court
is bound to issue it at the instance of a party aggrieved by the order of an inferior
tribunal except in cases where he has disentitled himself to the discretionary relief
by reason of his own conduct, submitting to jurisdiction, laches, undue delay or
waiver The proposition that the Application for Writ must be sought as soon as
injury is caused is merely an Application of the equitable doctrine that delay
defeats equity and the longer the injured person sleeps over his rights without
any reasonable excuse the chance of his success in Writ Application dwindles
and the Court may reject a Writ Application on the ground of unexplained delay.
An Application for a Writ of Certiorari should be filled within a reasonable time"
In Sarath Hulangamuwa Sriwardena Vs. The Principal Vishaka Vidyalaya
(1986-1 SLR-275), the Court of Appeal held that

--- Page 5 ---
the Writs are extraordinary remedies granted to obtain speedy relief under
exceptional circumstances and time is of the essence of the Application. The
laches of the Petitioner must necessarily be a determining factor in deciding the
Application for Writ as the Court will not lend itself to making a stultifying
order which cannot be carried out.
The foregoing line of authorities are united in deciding that delay and laches
are the most significant aspects to be considered in Writ Applications. If there
is a delay and laches on the part of the Petitioner, which has not been explained
to the satisfaction of the Court, the Court will not issue prerogative Writs.
On this premise, I am of the view that the instant application is liable to be
dismissed on this ground alone.
Moreover, the Petitioner in paragraph 15 of the Petition states that there was a
difficulty in ascertaining whether the land claimed by him is in fact was within
the land that had been acquired. In paragraph 27 (d) of the Petition, the
Petitioner further states that there is no proper demarcation or identification of
the land that he claimed. In this respect, it appears to this Court that the major
facts, namely the identification of the subject matter are in dispute in this
application. It is settled law that when the major facts are in dispute, until and
unless those facts are established in a trial Court, a Writ Court cannot exercise
its writ jurisdiction.
In this regard, I refer to the judgment of Thajudeen Vs. Sri-Lanka Tea Board
(1981-2SLR-471) where the Court of Appeal held that
where the major facts are in dispute and the legal result of the facts is subject to
controversy and it is necessary that the questions should be canvassed in a suit
where parties would have ample opportunity of examining the witnesses so that
the Court would be better able to judge which version is correct, a Writ will not
issue. Mandamus is pre-eminently a discretionary remedy. It is an
extraordinary, residuary and suppletory remedy to be granted only when there
are no other means of obtaining justice. Even though all other requirements for
securing the remedy have been satisfied by the applicant, the Court will decline
to exercise its discretion in his favour if a specific alternative remedy like a
regular action equally convenient, beneficial, and effective is available.
A.S. CHOUDRI in his book on the law of Writs and Fundamental Rights (2nd
Edition) Vol-2. At page 449) states that
where facts are in dispute and in order to get at the truth, it is necessary that
the question should be canvassed in a suit where parties would have amble

--- Page 6 ---
opportunity of examining their witnesses and the Court would be better able to
judge which version is correct, a Writ will not issue.
The Supreme Court in Francis Kulasooriya Vs. OIC- Police Station-
Kirindiwela ( SC Appeal No. 52/2021. SC Minute of 142023) observed that
Courts are reluctant to grant orders in the nature of writs when the matters on
which the relief is claimed are in dispute or in other words when the facts are in
dispute.
Thus, it is the considered view of this Court that the Petitioner in this
application is not entitled to the relief as prayed for as the major facts are in
dispute.
It is evident that the acquisition process was concluded in 1987, and thereby the
state became the owner of the land in dispute. Thereupon, the said land was
vested with the 2nd Respondent in terms of the provisions of the said Act. At
this juncture, it is pertinent to note that the Petitioner had purchased the subject
land from the 6th Respondent in 2020 by deed marked as P23a. In these
circumstances, it is abundantly clear that the Petitioner had not obtained title to
the subject matter by P2a, as the purported vendor of the said deed (6th
Respondent) did not have title to the same to execute a deed of transfer in
favour of the Petitioner. As such, the Petitioner has no locus standi to file and
maintain this application. The purported title of the Petitioner pleaded in the
Petition to the subject land is bad in law. In paragraphs 20, 21 and 23 of the
Petition, the Petitioner has admitted the fact that he had been deceived by the
6th Respondent pertaining to this transaction, and therefore he has taken action
against the 6th Respondent.
In this scenario, it is manifestly clear that the Petitioner is not entitled to
question whether the subject matter has been utilized for the purpose that it
had been acquired, whether the compensation had been paid, whether the steps
taken under the provisions of the said Act are lawful and/or whether the
subject matter is liable to be divested as the has not obtain title to the land in
dispute. The purported title deed produced by the Petitioner (P2a) is an illegal
and fraudulent document, and therefore, he is not entitled to the relief as
prayed for in the prayers to the Petition.

--- Page 7 ---
For the foregoing reasons, I hold that the Petitioner is not entitled to the relief
as prayed for. Thus, the Petition is liable to be dismissed.
Application dismissed. No costs.
