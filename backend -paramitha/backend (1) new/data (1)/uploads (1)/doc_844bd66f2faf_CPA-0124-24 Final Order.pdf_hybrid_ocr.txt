Sampath B. Abayakoon, J.
This is an application preferred by the 1st accused-petitioner (hereinafter referred
to as the petitioner) in terms of Article 138 of The Constitution seeking to invoke
the revisionary jurisdiction of this Court.
The petitioner is the 1st accused in the High Court of Ampara Case No.
HC/AMP/1976/19.

--- Page 4 ---
He has been indicted by the Hon. Attorney General along with two others, for
conspiring to cause criminal breach of trust to Seylan Bank of which he was an
employee attached to its Ampara Branch for a sum of Rs. 5019000/- between
the period of 142010 and 172010, and thereby committing an offence
punishable in terms of section 391 read with section 113B and 102 of the Penal
Code.
He has also been indicted for committing the earlier mentioned criminal breach
of trust for the said sum at the same time and at the same transaction, and
thereby committing an offence punishable in terms of section 391 of the Penal
Code.
The trial against the petitioner and the other accused has commenced before the
learned High Court Judge of Ampara and PW-01 has so far partly concluded his
evidence in chief. In the process of the said evidence, PW-01 has produced
several gold jewellery pawn receipts relevant to the action, marked up to P-04.
In the petition before the Court, the petitioner has sought to set aside the orders
given by the learned High Court Judge of Ampara allowing to mark documents
P-01, P-02, P-03, P-04, P-04(1), P-04(2), P-04(3), P04(4), P-04(5), P-04(6), P-
04(7), P-04(8), P-04(9), P-04(10), and the documents marked during the trial on
262023 and 222024, among other reliefs sought in the prayer of the
petition.
When this matter was supported for notice and also for a stay order, the main
reason submitted by the learned Counsel for the petitioner for initiating this
action was that the documents mentioned in the indictment filed by the Hon.
Attorney General are documents not tendered to Magistrates Court when the
matter was initially reported to the Magistrate of Ampara, and therefore
producing them at the High Court is against the provisions of section 431 of the
Code of Criminal Procedure Act.
It was his submission that the learned High Court Judge unfairly overruled his
objection and therefore, his client was denied a fair trial. It was also submitted

--- Page 5 ---
that although the learned High Court Judge ordered the prosecution to hand
over the copies of the items mentioned in the indictment as productions, the
prosecution has failed to hand over them. Another matter taken up by the
learned Counsel was that the pawn receipts so far marked at the trial are not
the originals, but photocopies.
It was on that basis the learned Counsel submitted that the petitioner has
sufficient exceptional grounds to invoke the revisionary jurisdiction of this Court
to seek relief as prayed for.
Having considered the submissions of the learned Counsel, this Court is of the
view that section 431 of the Code of Criminal Procedure Act has no direct
application to the matter under consideration.
It is clear from the proceedings of the High Court that the petitioner and the
other accused have been indicted before the High Court of Ampara for conspiracy
to commit criminal breach of trust, committing criminal breach of trust and also
aiding and abetting to commit criminal breach of trust, and thereby, causing a
loss to Seylan Bank of the sum mentioned in the indictment.
The evidence so far led appears to be on the basis that this criminal breach of
trust has been committed by knowingly accepting fake gold jewellery as pawn
articles to the bank. The documents that had been so far marked are the
duplicate copies of the pawn receipts the bank had in possession. Although the
learned Counsel claims that the prosecution has failed to mark the original
documents, obviously the originals of the pawn receipts cannot be in the
possession of the bank as the said originals are meant to be handed over to the
person who pawned the articles. The evidence of PW-01 led so far reveals the fact
that once a person pawned an item, a receipt is prepared in triplicate, where the
original is handed over to the customer, while the pink-coloured copy is kept
with the pawned article, and the green-coloured copy is used for auditing
purposes.

--- Page 6 ---
It is also clear from the proceedings that attempts had been made on behalf of
the accused to prevent the relevant documents being marked on unacceptable
grounds, where the learned High Court Judge has given a clear order in that
regard on 092023, which I find is a correct order made in that regard.
It appears from the proceedings that the questioned items of the fake gold
jewellery and the relevant pink and green coloured receipts issued in that regard
have been kept under the custody of the Seylan bank, being the owner of the
said items for better safekeeping. I find that this is a common practice adopted
by the Court to allow the owners of the items of productions to keep the custody
of the said productions until a conclusion of a trial for obvious reasons.
Although the learned High Court Judge has ordered that the items of jewellery,
which are the productions of the case shall be handed over to the Court, I do
not find any necessity to hand over the said jewellery to the Court as the purpose
is to ensure the safe custody of the same until the conclusion of the trial. In my
view, Seylan Bank is the best place where the said jewellery can be safely kept
for the purposes of this action as they are the owners of the jewellery.
As clearly determined by the learned High Court Judge, if the marked documents
or other productions are not in order or suspicious, it is a matter for the learned
trial Judge to determine on that when relevant. I find no basis to shut out such
documents or items of productions in the manner the petitioner is attempting to
claim.
It is clear from the case record that the learned trial Judge has done all he could
to ensure a fair trial towards the petitioner and the other accused in the case.
If the prosecution has failed to hand over copies of the other documents they
intend to mark at the trial as ordered by the learned High Court Judge, it will
again be a matter that the learned High Court Judge should decide whether to
allow evidence in that regard or what order should be made in that regard.

--- Page 7 ---
Even after the documents had been marked, the defence can again ask for copies
of the same, if they are unable to cross-examine the witnesses straightaway in
that regard, and obtain an order from the Court.
I am of the view that this type of applications should not be encouraged under
any circumstances, unless the decisions made by the trial Court in the process
of a trial are manifestly wrong, which shocks the conscience of the Court as it
would lead to delay in proceedings before a trial Court. In the instant matter, I
find no reasons to come to such a finding,
Having considered the application before the Court, and the relevant facts and
circumstances, I find that this is not a case where notice should be issued to the
named respondents, as I find no merit in any of the grounds urged before the
Court.
Accordingly, the revision application is dismissed without the notice being
issued.
The Registrar of the Court is directed to communicate this order to the High
Court of Ampara for necessary information.
