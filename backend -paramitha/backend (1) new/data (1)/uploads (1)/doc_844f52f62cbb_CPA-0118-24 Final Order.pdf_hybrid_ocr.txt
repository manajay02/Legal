Sampath B. Abayakoon, J.
This is an application preferred by the claimant-petitioner-petitioner (hereinafter
referred to as the petitioner) seeking to invoke the discretionary remedy of
revision granted to this Court by Article 138 of The Constitution.
This matter was supported for notice on the respondent-respondents mentioned
in the petition, and also for an order staying the operation of the order dated 112024 by the learned High Court Judge of the Provincial High Court of Central
Province Holden in Matale, and also the order dated 052019 by the learned
Magistrate of Matale, until the final conclusion of this application.
The learned State Counsel who represented the Hon. Attorney General as well
as the complainant-respondent-respondent objected to the issuance of formal
notice in this regard and also the stay orders as sought on the basis that there
exist no obvious exceptional grounds for this Court to issue notice.
This is a matter where the accused person mentioned in the petition has been
charged before the Magistrates Court of Matale in Case Number 56190 for
having transported Mahogany and Gini Sapu timber valued at Rs. 62,717.92/=
in the vehicle bearing number 226-9439 without having a valid permit in terms
of the Forest Ordinance, which is an offence punishable in terms of section 25(2)
read with section 40 of the Forest Ordinance as amended.
The accused mentioned has pleaded guilty to the charge before the learned
Magistrate of Matale, and subsequently, has been sentenced.
Thereafter, the learned Magistrate of Matale has called upon the registered owner
of the vehicle involved in the offence to show cause as to why the vehicle should
not be confiscated in terms of section 40 of the Forest Ordinance.
At the inquiry held in that regard, the petitioner, being the registered owner of
the vehicle, has given evidence and another witness has also been called upon
to give evidence on behalf of the registered owner.

--- Page 5 ---
At the conclusion of the inquiry, the learned Magistrate of Matale, of his order
dated 052019, has decided to confiscate the vehicle involved in the offence
on the basis that the registered owner has failed to establish that she has taken
the necessary precautions to prevent the offence being committed, and also that
she has had the knowledge of the accused committing the offence.
The petitioner has preferred an application in revision seeking to challenge the
said order before the Provincial High Court of Central Province Holden in Matale.
The learned High Court Judge of Matale of his judgment dated 112024 has
dismissed the revision application of the petitioner on the basis that he finds no
reason to interfere with the order of the learned Magistrate of Matale.
The petitioner has preferred the instant application before this Court seeking to
challenge the order of the learned Magistrate of Matale, as well as the judgment
of the learned High Court Judge of the Provincial High Court of Central Province
Holden in Matale.
It is settled law that the Court of Appeal will intervene using its power of granting
the discretionary remedy of revision only under exceptional circumstances.
I would like to quote from the case of Sadi Banda Vs. Officer-In-Charge of
Norton Bridge Police Station (2014) 1 SLR 33, which I find relevant in the
above context.
The revisionary power of Court is a discretionary power. This is an
extraordinary jurisdiction which is exercised by the Court and the grant of
relief is entirely dependent of the Court. The grant of such relief is of course
a matter entirely in the discretion of the Court, and always be dependent on
the circumstances of each case. Existence of exceptional circumstances is
the process by which the extraordinary power of revision should be adopted.
The exceptional circumstances would vary from case to case and their
degree of exceptionality must be correctly assessed and gauged by the Court
taking into consideration all antecedent circumstances using the yardstick

--- Page 6 ---
whether a failure of justice would occur unless revisionary powers are
invoked.
It was held in the case of Hotel Galaxy (Pvt) Ltd Vs. Mercantile Hotels
Management Ltd (1987) 1 SLR 5 that;
It is settled law that the exercise of the revisionary powers of the appellate
Court is confined to cases in which exceptional circumstances exist
warranting its intervention.
In the case of Wijesinghe Vs. Thamararatnam (Sriskantha Law Report
Volume IV page 47), it was stated that;
Revision is a discretionary remedy and will not be available unless the
application discloses circumstances which shocks the conscience of the Court.
In the case of Vanik Incorporation Ltd Vs. Jayasekare (1997) 2 SLR 365, it
was observed that;
Revisionary powers should be exercised where a miscarriage of justice has
occasioned due to a fundamental rule of procedure being violated, but only
when a strong case is made out amounting to a positive miscarriage of justice.
It was held in the case of Rustom Vs. Hapangama and Co. 1978/79 2 SLR 225
that;
Considering the facts and circumstances of the present case there are no
such exceptional circumstances disclosed as would cause the Appellate
Court to exercise its discretion and grant relief by way of revision. Unless
there was something illegal about the order made by the trial judge which
has deprived the petitioner of some right, the justice of the case required that
the Court of Appeal would not in the circumstances of this case grant the
petitioner the indulgence of exercising its revisionary powers.

The proviso of section 40 of the Forest Ordinance as amended by Forest
(Amendment) Act No. , under which the learned Magistrate of Matale
has allowed the petitioner to show cause as to why the vehicle involved in this
incident, namely the vehicle numbered 226-9439, should not be confiscated
reads as follows:
Provided that in any case where the owner of such tools, vehicles,
implements and machines used in the commission of such offence is
a third party, no order of confiscation shall be made if such owner
proves to the satisfaction of the Court that he has taken all
precautions to prevent the use of such tools, vehicles, implements,
cattle and machines, as the case may be, for the commission of the
offence.
Our Superior Courts have held the constant view that all precautions stated in
the section does not mean every possible precaution, but precautions that could
have been taken under the given facts and the circumstances. It is well-settled
law that in an inquiry of this nature, it is incumbent upon the owner of the
vehicle to satisfy the Court that he or she took reasonably possible precautions
to prevent the commission of the offence, and also had no knowledge of such an
offence being committed.
As correctly determined by the learned Magistrate of Matale, as well as the
learned High Court Judge of Matale, the petitioner giving evidence before the
Magistrates Court has clearly admitted that she had the knowledge of the timber
being transported in the vehicle belonging to her. The driver of the vehicle,
namely the accused, who was charged before the Magistrates Court has
informed her of the fact of loading timber to the vehicle to be transported
elsewhere.
There is nothing in the evidence to show that the petitioner took any steps to
inquire from the driver whether he is going to transport timber that is permitted

--- Page 8 ---
to be transported without a permit or at least what kind of timber he is
transporting and its destination.
This shows that the petitioner clearly had the knowledge of the offence being
committed and also has failed to take reasonable precautions to prevent the
offence from being committed.
Hence, it is the view of this Court that the learned Magistrate of Matale was
correct in confiscating the vehicle, and the learned High Court Judge of Matale
was correct in determining that he has no reasons to interfere by exercising the
revisionary jurisdiction granted to the said High Court in terms of Article 154P
of The Constitution.
For the reasons as considered above, this Court finds no reason to issue a formal
notice in relation to this application or to grant stay orders as sought by the
petitioner.
Accordingly, the application is dismissed without the formal notice being issued.
The Registrar of the Court is directed to communicate this order to the Provincial
High Court of the Central Province Holden in Matale, and also to the Magistrates
Court of Matale for information.
