K.M.G.H. KULATUNGA, J.
1. This application for bail is preferred by the Suspect-Petitioner under
the provisions of Section 83(2) of the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous
Drugs Ordinance. According to the Petition and the submissions
made during the inquiry, the suspect had been arrested on
21.05.2022. As stated in the annexed B report the suspect at the
point of arrest had been in possession of 25.6g of heroine. Then

--- Page 3 ---
upon being interrogated a further 144.5g of heroine had been
recovered from a house in Hokandara. It is in respect of these
recoveries that the suspect is now held in remand. According to the
Government Analysts Report the pure quantities of heroine in the
said two recoveries are 13.8g and 89.27g respectively. The
Government Analysts Report is dated 31.08.2022.
2. The main ground urged by Ms. Eranga Sirisena, Attorney-at- Law
for the Petitioner is that no indictment or charges have been
preferred up until the date of this inquiry. Accordingly, for almost
two and half years the Respondents have failed to institute criminal
action and prefer charges in respect of this matter. The learned
Counsel for the Petitioner admits that the suspect has another
pending case for a similar offence involving 79mg of heroine. The
main thrust of the argument advanced is that the delay of almost
two and a half-year without indicting or preferring charges is
excessive and oppressive which can be considered as an exceptional
circumstance.
3. In response and in opposition to this application, the learned State
Counsel Mr. Malik Azeez submitted that the cumulative quantity of
the two recoveries is 106g of pure heroine which according to Mr.
Azeez is substantial and considerable. Further, he submitted that
the IB extracts was received by the Hon. Attorney General almost
one year ago. The indictment is under consideration and it is now
finalized and a decision had been taken to send out two indictments
for each recovery separately. Mr. Azeez was optimistic that the
indictment will be dispatched soon. He also emphasized the fact that
the offence had been committed whilst the suspect was on bail for
another similar offence which is the matter involving 79mg of
heroine. It is the position of learned State Counsel that IBs have

--- Page 4 ---
been forwarded within one year and within another year
preparations have been made to finalized and forward an
indictment. In these circumstances, Mr. Azeez believes and submits
that the delay of two and half years cannot be considered as being
excessive or oppressive.
4. Upon been inquired, the learned State Counsel conceded that apart
from initial recoveries no further investigations were required or
directed by the Attorney General nor done by the Police. Therefore,
what was required to institute legal process was to forward the
Information Book extracts to the Attorney General to consider such
material and draw up the indictment.
5. This process has taken 30 months or a period of two and half years.
There is no convincing or any acceptable reason to explain this lapse
of time and to justify the same.
6. The primary ground of seeking bail is the alleged undue delay which
is referred to as oppressive or excessive delay which according to the
petitioner is an exceptional circumstance. The legislature has in its
wisdom limited the granting of bail in respect of certain offences
under the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance only
upon satisfying Court of exceptional circumstances. Section 83 as
amended by the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs
(Amendment) Act, No. reads thus;
83. (1) Subject to the provisions of sections 84, 85 and
subsection (2) of this section, a person suspected or accused of
an offence under sections 54A and 54B of this Ordinance, shall
not be released on bail by the High Court except in exceptional
circumstances.
(2) Notwithstanding the provisions of sections 84 and 85,
a person suspected or accused of an -

--- Page 5 ---
(a) of which the pure quantity of the dangerous drug,
trafficked, imported, exported or possessed is ten
grammes or above in terms of the report issued by the
Government Analyst under section 77A; and
(b) which is punishable with death or life
imprisoment,[sic] shall not be released on bail except by
the Court of Appeal in exceptional circumstances.
(3) For the purposes of this section dangerous drug means
Morphine, Cocaine, Heroin and Methamphetamine..
7. No doubt, under the normal law at the pre-trial and pre-conviction
stages granting of bail is the rule and the refusal is the exception.
This is in accord with the presumption of innocence that which
prevails during the pendency of the trial. However, when exceptional
circumstances are by statute decreed as being a necessary
prerequisite, then the legislature has intended that, persons
accused of such offences be held in remand pending the
determination of such trials. Considering section 83(1) of the
Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance (prior to the
amendment) in Labukola Ange Wisin Gedera Ashani
Dhanushshika v. OIC Divisional Crimes Investigation Unit
Panadura [CA/PHC/APN/04/2016- CAM 06.10.2016] it was opined
thus;
The Petitioners first point is that the suspect is in remand
nearly for two years. The intention of the Legislature is to keep
in remand any person who is suspected of or accused of
possessing or trafficking heroine until the conclusion of the
case. The Section 83(1) of the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous
Drugs Ordinance express the intention of the Legislature. It is
enacted by the Parliament that No person suspected or
accused of an offence under section 54A or section 54B of this

--- Page 6 ---
Ordinance shall be released on bail, except by the High Court
in exceptional circumstances.
8. No doubt the provisions of section 83 has been enacted with the
intent and purpose of containing and curbing the growing drug
related offences. The Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs
Ordinance being a special statute could certainly prevail and
override the provisions of other more general statutes. In relation to
bail, section 83 has incorporated provisions which decrees in the
mandatory form that bail should not ordinarily and as a rule be
granted in cases involving serious drug offences under subsection
(1) of section 54A and section 54B except in exceptional
circumstances. When a special statute contains specific provisions
for granting of bail such provisions cannot be ignored whilst
considering such bail applications. Considering a similar issue in
the Indian case of Arun v. D. Pakyntein, 2009(7) SCALE 568,
(2009)16 SCC 496 it was opined that, where a statute confers drastic
powers and provides for stringent penal provisions including the
matters relating to grant of bail, the conditions precedent therefore
must be scrupulously complied with.
9. However, to my mind this extremely stringent provision of section 83
on granting of bail certainly cannot become or be allowed to be used
as a means to incarcerate an accused person without instituting
action or concluding the trial for an indefinite and unreasonably long
time without a just cause. Thus, time in remand when considered in
conjunction with the failure to take meaningful steps to institute
action or diligently prosecute to a finality in context may create the
circumstances that can be considered exceptional. The time spent in
remand cannot be considered in the abstract to determine if its
exceptional. Similarly, the normal effluxion of time between the
arrest and trial, cannot by itself amount to an exceptional

--- Page 7 ---
circumstance. If the delay is inordinate and unexplained, it may very
well be.
10. It is very relevant to be conscious and mindful that remanding of a
person denies his freedom and liberty. In reality holding a person in
custody for two and half years virtually and necessarily deprives such
person, two and half years of his life and living. Therefore, those who
are responsible for so denying the freedom and liberty are required
to act consciously and diligently to expeditiously process and
progress with necessary steps to file criminal proceedings. Any
dereliction of this obligation in whatever degree or any lackadaisical
attitude exhibited in this process should necessarily be viewed with
utmost seriousness.
11. In the instant matter, in view of the submissions made by the learned
State Counsel, the police had taken one year to have IB extracts
forwarded to the Attorney General. No acceptable reason is
forthcoming. Similarly, another one and half years has now been
taken to finalize the charges or the indictment. I regret to note that
the persons empowered to take action to institute criminal
proceedings seem to have completely lost sight that a suspect is
languishing in remand somewhere. Further, it is admitted that a
previous application for bail had also been preferred by this suspect
which had been refused and rejected on 04.04.2024. This is deposed
to in paragraph 09 of the affidavit of the 2nd Respondent. The
relevance and what emanates from this fact is that the respondents
have been aware and it has been brought to their conscious attention
of the delay in finalizing criminal action in respect of this matter as
far back as 04.04.202. The said bail application bears No.
CA/BAL/409/2023 according to which in 2023 the Respondents
have had notice of this matter pending without criminal action as at

--- Page 8 ---
that point. No doubt, this Court has on 04.04.2024 dismissed the
said application. Now, at the end of November 2024, the indictment
is yet to be finalized and sent out. From April 2024 up to now the
State has very conveniently and nonchalantly let 07 months pass by
and suddenly when this matter was taken up for inquiry on
29.11.2024 informs that indictment will be sent out in due course.
This sequence of events in no uncertain terms demonstrate an
extreme indifference and disregard of the rights of a person in
remand. To our mind this lackadaisical attitude of those responsible
is serious. End of the day delay remains unexplained.
12. This suspect may have a similar pending matter and the quantities of
heroine is no doubt substantial and considerable. In my mind even
the worst criminal is entitled benefits and rights guaranteed and
protected by the constitution and statute. Where the delay is
unexplained and excessive the combined effect specially as the
Respondents in this instance having had sufficient notice in view of
the previous bail application should necessarily have consciously
acted and expeditiously indicted the suspect, which they have failed
to do.
13. Accordingly, as stated above in the absence of any valid and
acceptable reason the lapse of two and half years necessarily become
excessive and as far as the suspect is concerned it is oppressive. As
regards the lapse of 19 months, what may be exceptional is not the
per se lapse of time, but the excessive or oppressive delay. As held in
Attorney General v. Ediriweera (S.C. Appeal No. 100/2005), Delay
is always a relative term and the question to be considered is not
whether there was mere explicable delay, as when there is a backlog
of cases, but whether there has been excessive or oppressive delay and
this always depends on the facts and circumstances of the case.

--- Page 9 ---
14. Accordingly, I hold that in these circumstances the delay is excessive
and oppressive. And therefore, can be considered as been exceptional.
Accordingly, this Court is inclined to grant bail to the Petitioner-
Suspect to the following conditions.
i. Cash bail of Rs.200,000.00,
ii. Should produce two sureties who should execute bond in a
sum of Two Million Rupees each,
iii. Each surety should tender Grama Sevaka Certificates and
Police Reports from their respective residential area,
iv. The Suspect-Petition should report to the Officer-in-Charge of
the Antivice Striking Force, Walana Central, Panadura on
every 2nd and 4th Sunday of the month before 12 noon,
v. The Suspect-Petitioner is required to surrender his passport
or any travel document if he has any to the Registrar of the
Magistrates Court of Kaduwela.
15. Accordingly, this application is allowed and bail is granted to the Suspect-
Petitioner subject to the above conditions. Application is allowed. The
Registrar of this Court is allowed to send copy of this order to the Registrar
of the Magistrates Court of Kaduwela.
