K.M.G.H KULATUNGA, J.
1. The Petitioner is seeking bail for her husband Mohommad Nizar
Mohomadu Faizer, the Suspect. This Application is made under Section 83
(2) of the Poisons, Opium, and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance. According to
the Petitioner, the Suspect was arrested on 01.11.2022, with 32.28
grammes of pure heroin. The main ground urged is the discrepancy as to
the manner and place of finding the narcotics. It is submitted that,
according to the first B report, dated 02.11.2022, it is merely stated that
the heroin was found in the possession of the Suspect. However, in the
second B report dated 15.11.2022, it is stated that, according to the notes,
the said quantity of heroin was found in a three-wheeler. In both the B
reports the fact that a three-wheeler bearing number JT 1342 being taken
into custody is stated. Based on these two reports, it is submitted that
there is a serious doubt as to the precise manner and place of recovering
the heroin.
2. Accordingly, the Counsel for the Petitioner moves that this be considered
as being exceptional and bail be granted to the Suspect. It is also submitted
that the Suspect had been in remand for two years, and that too be
considered along with the said inconsistency arising out of the B reports
in respect of the manner of arrest and the detection. The learned State
Counsel objecting to this application submitted that the Suspect has
already been indicted, and the pure quantity of heroin is over 32 grammes.

It is his argument that there is no inconsistency in the B reports. The
State Counsel argues that the first B report, whilst stating that the heroin
was recovered from the possession of the Suspect opposite the main gate
of Peoples Park it also states that a three-wheeler was also taken into
custody. As regards the second B report, the State Counsel admits that
there is a difference in the form of narrating which specifically states that
the heroin was found in the three-wheeler. His submission is that when
heroin is found in the three-wheeler in which the accused was in and
owned by him it for all purposes is in his possession and what is stated in
both the reports is just that, and this is a matter to be considered by the
Trial Judge.
3. Under the provisions of Section 83 (2) of the Poisons, Opium, and
Dangerous Drugs Ordinance, it is only upon satisfying this Court of the
existence of exceptional circumstances that bail may be considered. If
there is a glaring inconsistency which throws an appreciable doubt as to
the manner of the detection, this Court has time and again considered it
as being exceptional. The rationale as I see there being no reasonable
prospect of the prosecution being able to prove the case at the end of the
trial. The matter that may be considered at the bail stage should be in the
nature of a glaring discrepancy or issue that is evident on the face of the
material.
4. According to the B reports in the instant application, the involvement and
recovery of a three-wheeler is adverted to in both. On the perusal of the
certified copy, I observe that the registered owner of this three-wheeler is
the Suspect himself (vide Certificate of Registration at page 13). Further,
this had been subsequently released to the wife of the Suspect. It is
apparent that the Suspect had been arrested opposite the Peoples Park
complex main entrance when he was with his three-wheeler. If the narcotics
was found in the three-wheeler of which the Suspect was the driver, and
any substance found in the three-wheeler may be considered to be in his
