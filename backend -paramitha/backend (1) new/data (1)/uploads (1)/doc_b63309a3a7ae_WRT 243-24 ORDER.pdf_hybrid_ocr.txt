This order pertains to whether to issue notice and to grant interim reliefs.
The Petitioner instituted this action by petition dated 08.04.2024 seeking the following
reliefs;
a. Issue notice of this Application on the respondents
b. Issue an order in the nature of a Writ of Certiorari quashing the notification marked
P3, P15 and P16 marked with the petition
c. Issue an interim order staying the proceedings of the said purported Arbitration No.
A/87/2023 until the final determination of this application.
d. Grant costs; and
e. Grant such other and further relief as to your lordships Court may seem meet.
The factual matrix concerned in this case is as follows:
According to the petition, the Petitioner is a company with limited liability and registered
under the Companies Act No. . The Petitioner states that the Petitioner and the 6th
Respondent Union entered into Collective Agreements from time to time and last of which
was signed on 12.12.2014 effective for a period of 3 years from 01.06.2014 to 31.05.2017 which
is marked as P2. However, the Petitioner by letter dated 11.01.2021 repudiated the said
Collective Agreement acting in terms of the Clause 5.1 of the Agreement and in terms of the
decision of the Board of Directors in the context of the dire financial crisis faced by the
Company. The Petitioner further states that the 6th Respondent in its complaint relied on the

--- Page 4 ---
said Agreement which is repudiated and has misled the 1st to 3rd Respondents that the said
Agreement is applicable to the dispute.
The Petitioner avers that the purported dispute that was referred to arbitration ia based on
the allegation made by the 6th Respondent that the Petitioner was obliged to calculate the
productivity pay in the United States Dollars and pay in Sri Lankan Rupees to the members
of the 6th Respondent Union and that such exchange rate to be determined at the prevalent
exchange of the Central Bank of Sri Lanka. According to the Petitioner, the said productivity
pay was made available to the members of the 6th Respondent as set out in the said
repudiated Collective Agreement and according to such Agreement, the exchange rate on
which the said productivity pay was to be calculated on Internal exchange rates published
by the Company each month.
The Petitioner states that in or around 20.07.2022, the 6th Respondent lodged a complaint
marked as P5 against the Petitioner to the Commissioner of Labour (Industrial Relations)
alleging inter alia that the productivity pay of the members of the 6th Respondent had been
reduced contrary to Annexure A of the Agreement marked P2. Further, by letter dated
24.11.2021 marked as P6, the 6th Respondent raised the same issue to the former Minister of
Labour. The Petitioner further states that as a result of the pandemic and the consequent
grounding of all aircrafts, the Petitioner was compelled to take steps to mitigate its losses
and as such a decision was reached under the auspices of the then Minister of Skills and
Development and Employment and Labour Relations on 04.05.2020 to permit inter alia the
reduction of salaries etc.
The Petitioner states that after several rounds of discussions pertaining to the 6th
Respondents complaint, an inquiry was held on 03.03.2022 chaired by the Commissioner of
Labour (Industrial Relations). Further, the Petitioner states that the Employers Federation
of Ceylon on behalf of the Petitioner set out the position of the Petitioner as a response to the
documents marked P5 and P6 and requested the 3rd Respondent to conclude the matter.
According to the Petitioner, the 6th Respondent by letter dated 15.02.2023 marked as P11
moved the Commissioner of Labour (Industrial Relations) to refer the purported dispute to
arbitration. Thereafter, the 3rd Respondent by letter dated 07.07.2023 marked as P12
informed the Petitioner that in the absence of a resolution to the purported dispute, the said
matter has been referred to arbitration under Section 4 (1) of the Industrial Disputes Act No.

--- Page 5 ---
as amended (hereinafter referred to as the Act). As per the Notice dated 20.10.2023
marked as P13, the dispute sough to be referred to arbitration was;
Whether the Flight Attendants of Sri Lankan Airlines Limited has been caused
injustice in the receipt of productivity pay in Sri Lankan rupees by an exchange rate
lesser than the rate determined by the Central bank of Sri Lanka for USA Dollars
being used and if so, to what relief they are entitled.
The Petitioner received a Notice dated 17.11.2023 signed by the 2nd Respondent informing
that as per the powers vested in the 1st Respondent, the Minister under Section 4(1) of the
Act, the 4th Respondent was appointed as the Arbitrator and a further Notice was received
by the Petitioner dated 30.11.2023 signed by the 1st Respondent appointing the 4th
Respondent as the Arbitrator.
The Notice dated 11.12.2023 marked as P16 informed the Petitioner that the purported
Arbitration has been commenced by the 1st Respondent under No. A/87/2023.
The main contention of the Petitioner is that the decision of the 1st Respondent to refer the
purported dispute to Arbitration is unlawful and ultra vires.
In this context, the Petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking inter
alia a writ of Certiorari quashing the Notifications marked P13, P14, P15 and P16.
This Court notes that the primary gravamen of the Petitioner is that the 1st Respondent has
failed to act in terms of Section 4 (1) of the Act when referring the dispute for arbitration.
For easy reference, Section 4 (1) of the Act is reproduced below.
The Minister may, if he is of the opinion that an industrial dispute is a minor dispute,
refer it, by an order in writing, for settlement by arbitration to an arbitrator appointed
by the Minister or to a labour tribunal, notwithstanding that the parties to such
dispute or their representatives do not consent to such reference.
According to the above Section, the Minister should form the opinion that the dispute
involved is a minor industrial dispute before referring such a dispute for arbitration.
This Court is mindful of the context in which this dispute arose. When the country was facing
the financial crisis, the exchange rate for dollars was beyond Rs. 400. In that context, the 6th

--- Page 6 ---
Respondent referred the matter to 3rd Respondent informing that there is a dispute. The fact
remains that the Petitioner was paying the particular productivity pay based on the internal
exchange rate of the Company. We are mindful that once the industrial dispute was referred
to the Arbitrator under Section 4 (1), he has to hear the evidence to the dispute and then
make an award. The Arbitrator has to act judicially in making the ultimate award which is
binding on the parties. Therefore, the opinion of the Minister with regard to whether there
is a minor industrial dispute is important. There is no indication in the Act as to what
constitutes a minor dispute. Thus, the Minister needs to form the opinion that the dispute is
a minor dispute. If not, the Minister can forward such dispute to Labour Court for settlement.
We are mindful that there was a Collective Agreement between the parties before the dispute
arose.
We are mindful that the 1st Respondent has failed to file any written submission or
Statements of Objection as to how the opinion was formed for this dispute by Minister.
Our Courts have considered Section 4(1) of the Act and broadly discussed the aspect of
forming the opinion by the Minister when referring a matter for arbitration.
In Volanka Ltd V. Seneviratne Minister Of Labour And Others (2000) 2 SLR 19 at 23  24,
His Lordship De Silva J held that;
Section 4 of the Act confers powers on the Minister to refer by an order in writing an
industrial dispute if he is of the opinion that the industrial dispute is a minor dispute
for settlement by an arbitrator appointed by him. The order of reference is an
administrative act of the Minister who has to form an opinion as to the factual
existence or apprehension of an industrial dispute (Aislaby Estate Ltd. Vs.
Weerasekara(1)). Justice Pathirana in the same case at page 253 stated that "I go still
further and take the view that section 4(1) of the Act vests the Minister with an
amplitude of power (subject only to the fetter that he is referring an industrial dispute
within the meaning of the Act) to order in writing once he is of the opinion that the
industrial dispute is a minor dispute for settlement by arbitration to the Labour
Tribunal . as the Minister is acting solely in an administrators capacity and not
judicially or quasi judicially. The concluding words in section 4(1) "notwithstanding
that the parties to such dispute or their representatives do not consent to such a

--- Page 7 ---
reference", in fact highlight the amplitude of power vested by section 4 in the
Minister".
In Standard Chartered Bank v. The Minister of Labour Relations and Manpower and Others
CA (Writ) Application No. 231/2009 decided on 17.07.2019, His Lordship Arjuna Obeyesekere
J held that;
Page 19,
It is the view of this Court that in terms of Section 4(1}, the Minister must form the
opinion that there exists an industrial dispute, prior to making any reference of such
dispute for resolution by arbitration. This Court is further of the view that where such
a reference is challenged, as in this application, the Minister must explain the matters
that were considered by him in forming such an opinion, or, as submitted by the
learned President's Counsel for the Petitioner, that the "Minister had applied a
judicial mind or even an objective mind, or with all due deference a sensible mind to
the matter".
Page 22,
This Court is of the view that in terms of Section 4(1) of the Industrial Disputes Act,
the Minister is not obliged to refer for arbitration, each and every dispute that he or
she is presented with. The Minister must look at each request objectively and exercise
his discretion judiciously. The fact that the Minister has a discretion is clearly
established when one considers that the Minister may refer for arbitration only if, "he
is of the opinion that an industrial dispute is a minor dispute". How does he form an
opinion that there exists an industrial dispute and that such dispute is a minor
dispute? Is it only by considering the argument advanced by the employee or should
the Minister also consider the position of the employer, as well as any other matters
that the Minister may consider relevant? This Court, whilst not subscribing to the
view that it is mandatory for the Minister to obtain the views of all parties prior to
making a reference under Section 4(1), takes the view that in this application, given
the background circumstances such as the inquiry under the TEW Act, and the
litigation challenging the findings of the Inquiry Officer, the Minister was under a
duty to consider the documents 'X10', 'XU' and 'X12' prior to forming an opinion that

--- Page 8 ---
there exists an industrial dispute. Unfortunately, the Minister has not done so, and
therefore it is the view of this Court that the Minister has failed to take into
consideration relevant matters, prior to arriving at a decision. The learned Senior
Deputy Solicitor General for the 1st and 2nd Respondents had submitted that in terms
of Section 4(1), the consent of the parties is not required for a reference and that this
is an indication that the Minister does not have to consult the other party, prior to
making the reference to arbitration. Whilst agreeing that the consent of the parties is
not required, this Court is of the view that this is not an indication that the Minister
is not required to address his mind in a judicial manner. If this Court may borrow the
language used by the Supreme Court in Municipal Council Colombo vs Munasinghe
with reference to the power of an arbitrator appointed under the Industrial Disputes
Act, the Minister or for that matter, no public official 'has the freedom of the wild
horse' to do whatever he or she wants to. All exercise of statutory duties and functions
must be within the four corners of the statute that confers such duties and functions,
and any transgression will be dealt with in terms of the law.
This was followed in Lanka Electricity Company (Private) Limited v. T. Piyasoma and
Others, CA (Writ) Application No: 310/2017, decided on 11.09.2020 at page 13, His Lordship
Arjuna Obeyesekere J held that;
There is one matter that I wish to advert to, even though the Petitioner has not
challenged the reference to arbitration. In terms of Section 4(1), the Minister must
form the opinion that there exists an industrial dispute, prior to making any reference
of such dispute for resolution by arbitration. The Minister would be guided in this
regard by the Commissioner General of Labour who therefore has the duty to carefully
consider if an industrial dispute has been made out by the complainant.
In the instant case, the 1st Respondent has failed to present any evidence before this Court
that he addressed his mind in a judicial manner to properly form the opinion that there is a
minor dispute existing before referring such dispute for arbitration.

--- Page 9 ---
In view of this, we hold that the Petitioner has made a prima facie case that the Ministers
reference in the instant case is subject to judicial review on the ground that such reference is
ultra vires the Section 4(1) of the Act.
Thus, we issue notice to the Respondents and allow the interim order prayed in prayer ( c) of
the petition dated 08.04.2023 until the conclusion of this application.
