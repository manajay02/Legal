B. Sasi Mahendran, J.
This order pertains to whether to issue notice and to grant interim reliefs.
The Petitioner instituted this application by petition dated 28.11.2022 praying for the
following reliefs:

--- Page 5 ---
a. Issue notice to the Respondents.
b. Issue a direction to the 23rd Respondent to submit to Your Lordships' Court
certified copy of Individual marking sheets used by the members of the Selection
Committee based on which the Evaluation Sheet marked P6c was prepared.
c. Issue a mandate in the nature of Writ of certiorari quashing:
i. the Evaluation process and assessment of candidates as depicted in
document marked as P6c adopted by the Selection Committee consisting
of the 02nd, 03rd, 11th, 12th, 18th and 22nd Respondents, and
ii. the decision made by the Council of the O1st Respondent on 27.11.2021
at its 251st meeting as stated in document marked as P6e to appoint the
24th, 25th, 26th and 27th Respondents to the Post of Lecturer
Probationary\ in Information and Communication Technology in the
Department of the Information and Communication Technology of the
Faculty of Technology in the O1st Respondent based on the
recommendation of the Selection Committee.
d. Direct the Chairman, University Grants Commission to conduct an appropriate
inquiry and take suitable disciplinary action against the members of the
Selection Committee and Council of the 01st Respondent who are responsible for
the process adopted in the evaluation of candidates in the interview and
unethical conduct in decision making process to make appointment to the Post
of Lecturer [Probationary] in Information and Communication Technology in the
Department of the Information and Communication Technology of the Faculty of
Technology in the 01st Respondent.
e. For Costs and,
f. For such further and other reliefs as to Your Lordships' Court shall seem meet.
The facts of this case are briefly as follows:

--- Page 6 ---
According to the Petition, the Petitioner has invoked the jurisdiction of this Court to quash
the evaluation process and assessment of candidates as depicted in the document marked
P6c adopted by the Selection Committee and quash the decision made by the Council on
27.11.2021 at its 251st Meeting to appoint the 24th to 27th Respondents for the post of
lecturer marked as P6e based on the recommendation of the Selection Committee.
According to the Petitioner, the 1st Respondent published a notice in the newspaper on
19.11.2020 marked as P3 calling for applications inter alia for the post of Lecturer
(Probationary) from suitable candidates. The Petitioner has made an application dated
14.12.2020 for the said post and was asked to be present for an interview on 24.11.2021
where she appeared before the Selection Committee.
The Petitioner further avers that she unofficially became aware that the Council of the 1st
Respondent had decided to recruit and appoint the 24th, 25th, 26th, and 27th Respondents
for the said post based on the recommendations of the Selection Committee on the basis
of the overall assessment of marks scored in the interview.
Since the Petitioner was not selected for the said post, she sought details of marks for
candidates and overall by her application dated 28.11.2021 from the Information Officer
of the 1st Respondent under the Right to Information Act No. which is marked
as P6a. The Petitioner was given a certified copy of the Evaluation Sheet marked as P6c
by the Information Officer by letter dated 29.11.2021. Further, for another application by
the Petitioner, a letter dated 29.12.2021 was received informing that as decided by the
Council in its 251st meeting, letters of appointment were issued to the 24th and 27th
Respondents and that the 24th, 26th, and 27th Respondents have assumed duties with effect
from 13.12.2021 with a copy of the Council Memo: SEU/C/2021/251/Addl/01.
The Petitioner further states that she became aware of the headings under which the
evaluation of the candidates when perusing the said Council Memo. The Petitioner avers

--- Page 7 ---
that when she requested further information by letter dated 13.12.2021, it was denied by
letter dated 23.12.2021 under Part II, Section 5 (1) (a) of the said Act. When the Petitioner
appealed to the 2nd Respondent about the said denial, by letter dated 30.12.2021 the
Petitioner was informed that the said request was denied under Part II, Section 5 (1) (a)
and (g) of the said Act. Further, the Petitioner states that, although several requests were
made to the Information Officer and the 2nd Respondent, she did not receive a satisfactory
response.
The Petitioner contends that, when the Evaluation Sheet marked P6c is carefully perused,
it becomes obvious that the Petitioner should have been given more scores based on her
graduate, postgraduate, and research qualifications and also for her experience working
as a Temporary Demonstrator. The Petitioner avers that, upon an appeal by the
Petitioner on 16.02.2022, the Right to Information Commission delivered its decision on
29.08.2022 holding that the 1st Respondent should release the information requested by
the Petitioner before 30.09.2022.
The Petitioner further avers that the Petitioner received a letter dated 29.09.2022 marked
as P10a from the 1st Respondent with the information requested by the Petitioner. The
Petitioner states that the annexures of P10a demonstrate that some of the selected
candidates were allocated marks in violation of the marking scheme disclosed by the 1st
Respondent. The Petitioner further contends that the 1st Respondent University has also
failed to adhere to the Sri Lanka Qualifications Framework (SLQF) to which the
University Grants Commission has given effect by Circular No. 05/2013(i) dated
25.08.2017 thereby advising the universities to follow the said SLQF in assessing the
qualification of candidates in providing employment in universities.

--- Page 8 ---
According to the Petitioner, the 1st Respondent also had breached Circular No. 721 which
contains Schemes of Recruitment in connection with employment of academic staff by
selecting and appointing the 26th Respondent in the said Post.
The Petitioner further avers that the 24th and 25th Respondents were selected and
appointed based on the personal relationships they have with the 2nd, 11th, and 12th
Respondents who evaluated, selected and recommended the said appointments.
The main gravamen of the Petitioner is that the selection process of the Selection
Committee and the decision process of the 1st Respondent to employ the 24th to 27th
Respondents are unlawful and unfair.
In this context, the Petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking inter
alia writs of Certiorari to quash the documents marked P6c and P6e on the basis that the
marks there was bias towards certain candidates.
2nd Respondent on 01.03.2023 filed the objection marking documents R1 to R11.
When the matter was supported on 23.10.2024 the Counsel of the Petitioner brought to
the notice of the Court that documents marked as R2A, R2A(a), (b), and (c) which were
filed by the 2nd Respondent, the said documents were prepared by the Selection
Committee for the interview held on 24.11.2022 indicating the marks obtained by the said
candidates. On the other hand, the documents received by the Petitioner through the
Information Officer is totally different. That is to say, the documents marked as R2A,
R2A(a), (b), and (c) have interpolations that were not authorized. The question arises as
to whether those were done at the interview or later. Even, no one has certified when
these alterations were done.
When perusing the documents, a doubt is created as to whether these corrections were
done by the Interview Panel or subsequently by any other person as alleged by the
Petitioner.

--- Page 9 ---
Considering the documents, the Petitioner has established a prima facie case to be
considered by this Court.
Therefore, we issue notice to the Respondents.
