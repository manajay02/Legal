ORDER
B. Sasi Mahendran, J.
This order pertains to whether to issue notice to the Respondents and grant interim
orders.
The Petitioner instituted this action by petition dated 18.08.2020 seeking inter alia writs
of Certiorari to quash the orders made by the minister pertaining to registration and
issuance of licences to tuskers and elephants 1991 contained in extraordinary gazette
notification bearing No. 662/4 dated 14.05.1991 marked "P-5" and the amended
indictment dated 02.12.2019 marked "p-3" in the case bearing No. HC (TAB) 504/19 of the
High Court-at-bar holden in Colombo.
The relevant facts of this case are as follows:
According to the petition, the Petitioner owns tamed/domesticated elephants and provides
such elephants to temples for Perehara and to government departments for their
functions. The Petitioner states that the Petitioner and 6 others were indicted before the
Permanent High Court-at-Bar holden in Colombo under the case bearing No.
HC/PTB/1/3/2018 by the indictment dated 08.10.2018 where the said indictment was
dismissed and the Accused were discharged by the Court.
The Petitioner further states that the Petitioner and 7 others were again indicted before
the High Court-at-Bar holden in Colombo by the indictment dated 22.07.2019 containing
36 charges in the case bearing No. HC (TAB) 504/19. As per the petition, the said

--- Page 5 ---
indictment was amended on 02.12.2019 where 28 charges levelled against all the Accused
pertaining to 5 elephants bearing registration Nos. 163, 164, 202, 303, and 350 which
were in the custody of the Petitioner at the time the said elephants were taken into the
custody of the Department of Wildlife Conservation.
The Petitioner further avers that during the prosecution trial on 16.12.2019, through the
witnessed marked PW 01, the prosecution marked an extraordinary gazette notification
bearing No. 662/4 dated 14.05.1991 marked as P5 which contains orders/regulation made
by the Minister in charge of the subject regarding registration and issuance of licences to
tuskers and elephants known as Order pertaining to registration and issuance of licences
to tuskers and elephants 1991. The Petitioner states that during the cross-examination,
no witness testified that the order in the said gazette notification had been presented to
the Parliament and approved by the Parliament. The Petitioner further states that the
witness marked PW 02 admitted that orders made under the provisions of the Fauna and
Flora Protection Ordinance as amended by Act No. and Act No.
(hereinafter referred to as the Ordinance) need to be approved by the Parliament in order
for the said orders to be valid and forceful.
The Petitioner states that however, the Learned Judges of the High Court-at-Bar
delivered its order dated 22.06.2020 marked as P8 inter alia holding that it is established
that the orders made by the Minister contained in the said gazette notification have been
approved by Parliament and that in any event when considering the provisions of the
Ordinance, the regulations contained in the said gazette notification are not material to
this case. This Court notes that this particular order was not challenged in this
application.
The Petitioner further states that the Learned Judges of the High Court-at-Bar delivered
another order on the same day acquitting the 3rd, 4th, 6th and 8th Accused and deciding to

call defense in relation to the 1st, 2nd, 5th and 7th Accused. Thereafter, the Accused appealed
against the said orders of the Learned Judges of the High Court-at-Bar. The Petitioner
further states that the 6th Respondent made an application under the Right to Information
Act No. to the Information Officer of the Parliament to ascertain whether the
orders made by the Minister contained in the said gazette notification have in fact been
approved by the Parliament.
The Petitioner further states that as per the charges contained in the said indictment, the
Petitioner has been charged under Section 22A (1) and 22A(7) of the Ordinance for having
in his custody elephants that have not been duly registered and in respect of which
licences have not been issued under the provisions of Ordinance and under Section 5 (3)
of the Public Property Act.
The main contention of the Petitioner is that the amended indictment dated 02.12.2019
marked P3 cannot be maintained against the Petitioner on the basis that the orders made
by the Minister in relation to registration and issuance to tuskers and elephants marked
P5 have not been presented to and approved by the Parliament, therefore are void ab
initio.
In this context, the Petitioner has invoked the writ jurisdiction of this Court seeking inter
alia writs of Certiorari to quash the order made by the Minister marked P5 and the
amended indictment dated 02.12.2019 marked P3.
At this juncture where a public document and its force in law is challenged, this Court is
mindful of Section 114 of the Evidence Ordinance and Illustration (d) to the said Section
which read as follows:
The Court may presume the existence of any fact which it thinks likely to have
happened, regard being had to the common course of natural events, human

Further, this Court is mindful of the prosecutorial discretion that the Attorney General is
conferred with to institute criminal proceedings by filing an indictment. Our Courts have
extensively discussed this principle in the following judgment;
In Mohamed Imad Ibthisam Fakhir v. Hon. Attorney General CA (Writ) Application No:
74/2021 decided on 26.03.2021, His Lordship Arjuna Obeyesekere, J (as he then was) held
that;
While the Attorney General has the discretion to institute criminal proceedings,
such discretion is generally referred to as the prosecutorial discretion in the
Common law jurisdictions, with the Attorney General or the Director of Public
Prosecution, as the case may be, being conferred with the prosecutorial powers to
institute criminal proceedings. It would therefore be appropriate to consider at the
outset whether the power of the Attorney General to forward indictment against
persons who are suspected to have committed an offence is amendable to the Writ
jurisdiction conferred on this Court by Article 140 of the Constitution, and if so,
the circumstances in which a Court will interfere with that power by way of judicial
review.
Further held;
A review of the English cases in this area show that while judicial review is
available in respect of the prosecutorial discretion in order to prevent any abuse
by the prosecuting authorities, the English Courts are mindful of its impact on the
criminal justice system, and has therefore clearly indicated its extreme reluctance
to intervene by way of judicial review.
A similar approach has been taken by the Supreme Court of our country in respect
of decisions of the Attorney General.

--- Page 9 ---
In light of the above judicial pronouncement, it is evident that the Honourable Attorney
General has the discretion to file indictments based on the law and the evidence available.
In the instant case, the main argument brought forward by the Petitioner is that the
amended indictment dated 02.12.2019 is not valid as the law on which such indictment
was based, has no force of law. Nevertheless, this Court accedes to the presumption laid
down in Section 114 of the Evidence Ordinance unless it is proven otherwise. When the
Honourable Attorney General decided to send the indictment, he relied on the particular
gazette which is in force. Therefore, the amended inditement filed by the Honourable
Attorney General exercising the prosecutorial discretion is in accordance with the law.
In view of the above reasoning, we dismiss the application.
No order for costs.
