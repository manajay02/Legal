K.M.G.H KULATUNGA, J.
1. This application is made under Section 83(2) of the Poisons, Opium and
Dangerous Drugs Ordinance seeking bail for the 2nd suspect, Thisaru
Eranda. According to the Petitioner the 2nd suspect was arrested on
18.02.2023 with 100.69g of pure Methamphetamine. The grounds urged
are the relative weakness of the case and the unusual delay in concluding
the pre-trial conference.
2. It is common ground that the indictment has been filed on 12.02.2024,
within one year of the arrest and detection. The main grievance and
complaint of the petitioner is the unusual delay in concluding the pre-trial
proceedings in the Galle High Court. It is argued that provisions of Section

--- Page 3 ---
195A. (5) requires the pre-trial conference to be concluded within 3 months.
Section 195A. (5) reads thus;
195A. (5) Every endeavor shall be made to conclude a pre-trial
conference as expeditiously as possible:
Provided however, a pre-trial conference shall be concluded
within a period not exceeding three months from the date on
which the accused appeared before the court or was
required to appear before the court.
3. Of course, on the face of it the proviso to section 195A. (5) does require the
pre-trial proceeding to be concluded within three months from the date on
which the accused appeared before the court. It is couched in the
mandatory form. However, this matter is now set to be mentioned on
30.01.2025. Two previous scheduled pre-trial conference dates have been
cancelled. The petitioner tendered a certified copy of the journal entries of
HC/5963/23 of the High Court of Galle. According to which the indictment
was served on 12.02.2024 and this matter was set for pre-trial on
25.04.2024and mentioned 19.06.2024 and now it is to be mentioned again
on 30.01.2025. Accordingly, from the point of serving the indictment it has
now taken almost ten months and the trial date is yet to be fixed. The matter
for determination and consideration is, whether this delay in these
circumstances will be exceptional for the purposes of considering bail under
Section 83(2) of the Poisons, Opium and Dangerous Drugs Ordinance. The
mere delay or the lapse of time does not amount to being exceptional unless
such delay is excessive or oppressive. Pre-trial conference is a joint exercise
with the participation of the prosecuting counsel, defence counsel and the
aggrieved party or the counsel if any, appearing on behalf of the aggrieved
party, of course presided over by the High Court Judge or the Recorder
Judge. Thus, it is the obligation of all the parties to endeavour to conclude
the same within the prescribed 3 month period though presided over by the
High Court Judge. In the present instance it appears from the journal
entries that the Accused himself has acquiesced and at least passively

--- Page 4 ---
participated in the cancellation of the pre-trial conference and the in the
postponement beyond 3 months without any objection.
4. Of course, the procedure prescribed by section 195A of the Code of Criminal
procedure Act does require a pre-trial conference to be concluded within a
period not exceeding three months from the date on which the accused
appeared before the court and it is primarily the duty of the presiding judge
to ensure the adherence to the said mandatory time frames. However, in
the circumstances of this matter the petitioner is not entitled take
advantage of and cannot be heard to say that the said delay is excessive or
oppressive.
5. The prosecution has expeditiously filed the indictment within a reasonable
time. However, the pre-trial proceedings appear to have taken time initially
due to not providing all the documents. Then, as to the delay in calling and
receiving certain items of productions and the other delay in this instance
cannot entirety or primarily be attributed to conduct of an oppressive or
excessive nature on the part of the prosecution. It may appear to be
excessive in the context of section 195A (5), but it is the Judge who had
granted these dates and certain steps to obtain the productions have taken
some time. Upon the consideration of the totality of the facts and
circumstances it appears that the time taken is not unusual and unique to
this matter alone and this appears to be the usual lapse of time that is
common to all similar cases pending before the said High Court. No doubt,
the 2nd suspect is said to have no previous convictions. However, since the
lapse of time in context cannot be singularly considered as being oppressive
or excessive, this cannot be considered as being exceptional.
